The Key press screen collects everything about the physical act of pressing a key: vibration, sound, the backspace swipe, the preview bubble, timing, and press-and-hold shortcuts. This page lists them in the order they appear on screen. It's the field reference. For the reasoning behind each option, read [Sound & vibration](/typing/feedback/) and [Typing basics](/typing/basics/) instead.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Key press" />

The Key press screen is a hub. Haptics and sound, Key popup and Press and hold shortcuts are pages of their own; Alternate keys and Timing stay on the hub.

### Haptics and sound

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Haptic feedback" />

| Setting | Control | Options / range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key press haptics | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Haptic style | Choice (chips) | Tap / Key / Click / Heavy / Sharp / Custom | Tap |
| Haptic strength *(Custom only)* | Slider | 5–60 ms | 15 ms |
| Haptic intensity *(Custom, Sharp)* | Slider | 1–255, shown as a percentage | 255 (100%) |
| Press and hold haptics | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Press and hold release haptics | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Cursor movement haptics *(shown only while Key press haptics is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Vibrate on space *(shown only while Key press haptics is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Vibrate on delete swipe *(same)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Vibrate on key repeat *(same)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Follow the system touch feedback setting *(same)* | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Vibrate past the system touch feedback switch *(same, Android 13+, hidden while the row above is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Mute haptics in Do Not Disturb *(same)* | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Click on key repeat *(shown only while Key sound is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |

**Key press haptics** is the master switch. Turn it off and the five per-event rows below it disappear rather than grey out.

**Haptic style** picks how the buzz is produced. Tap and Key hand it to the platform's own haptic call, which is what stock keyboards use, so it follows the vendor's tuning and the system haptic-intensity setting. Tap asks for the keyboard-specific waveform most vendors tune, and it's the default. Key asks for the general button press. Click and Heavy use a hardware-tuned effect (Android 10+). Sharp plays a hardware click primitive whose strength follows the intensity slider (Android 11+). Custom drives the vibration motor straight from the strength and intensity sliders. A style a device can't produce falls back to a hardware Click, then to Custom.

Two of the per-event rows are odd ones out. **Click on key repeat** has nothing to do with vibration at all: it's the sound half of "Vibrate on key repeat", playing the key sound on every auto-repeat while <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> or <KeyCap>Space</KeyCap> is held, and it does nothing unless Key sound is on. **Follow the system touch feedback setting** isn't a per-event gate either. It hands the master switch over to Android's own touch-vibration toggle. It ships off, so turning off touch feedback in Android's sound settings leaves the keyboard buzzing. Turn this on if you expect the system switch to cover the keyboard too. **Vibrate past the system touch feedback switch** extends that to the Custom, Sharp, Click and Heavy click styles, which Android otherwise silences with its switch; the System styles never needed it. When Android's switch is off, a note in the group says whether the chosen style still vibrates.

### Key press sound

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Key press sound" />

| Setting | Control | Options / range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key sound | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Sound style | Choice (chips) | Click / Standard / Pop / Thock / Chime / Custom / Sound pack | Click |
| Installed sound library *(Custom only)* | List + import | up to 30 installed sounds | Nothing installed |
| Installed sound packs *(Sound pack only)* | List + import | up to 20 installed packs | Nothing installed |
| Key release sound *(Sound pack only, and only for a pack that has key-up recordings)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| A pack per layout *(Sound pack only, with 2+ layouts enabled and a pack installed)* | List of layouts | Any installed pack, or "Same as the pack above" | Every layout follows the selected pack |
| Sound volume | Slider | 5%–100% | 50% |

Seven chips, not six. Click and Standard come from the device's own system sound pack; Pop, Thock and Chime are synthesized in-app and sound identical on every device. The last two both name something you've installed rather than a fixed waveform, so each reveals its own list when you pick it. Each also selects the first thing in that list as you tap the chip, so the keyboard changes sound straight away rather than clicking until you make a second choice. With nothing installed yet, the chip still selects: the list it reveals is where you install one.

Choosing **Custom** reveals your installed sound library and an **Import a sound file (.mp3)** button, and the label undersells it, since it also accepts `.ogg` and `.wav` files. The library holds up to 30 sounds between imports and installed sound addons.

Choosing **Sound pack** reveals a second, separate list with its own **Import a sound pack (.wmsoundpack)** button, holding up to 20 packs. A pack is one keyboard recorded many times over, up to 32 recordings per key, and the keyboard picks a different one per key press. Each pack's row says what it carries, so "10 recordings • keys sound on the way up" means the pack was recorded with key-up sounds as well, and "26 recordings • its own sound for 26 keys" means it recorded individual letters. For packs with key-up sounds only, a **Key release sound** toggle appears under the list. It's on by default. Packs without key-up recordings don't show it, because there'd be nothing for it to do. Tap a pack in the list and it plays a full keystroke rather than just the way down.

Below that, with two or more layouts enabled, **A pack per layout** lists your layouts so one of them can use a different pack from the rest; a layout you don't touch says "Same as the pack above". See [Sound & vibration](/typing/feedback/#sound-packs) for the longer version.

Installing a [Sound addon](/addons/overview/) highlights this **Sound style** row and offers to switch to it right after install, the same "Use it now?" flow other addon types use (see [Installing addons](/addons/installing/)).

A theme can carry a key sound of its own, and where it does, it wins over everything on this row, the same way it wins on colours. It only wins with a sound it can make, though: a theme naming a sound or pack this device doesn't have falls back to whatever is picked here. See [Font and sound](/themes/editor/#font-and-sound).

### Key popup

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Key popup" />

| Setting | Control | Options / range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key popup | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Popup on number pads *(shown only while Key popup is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Space above the keyboard *(same)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Minimum popup duration *(same)* | Slider | 0–300 ms | 140 ms |
| Popup on key *(shown only while Key popup is on)* | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Popup font size | Slider | ×0.70–×1.60 | ×1.00 |
| Popup height *(shown only while Key popup is on)* | Slider | 32–160 dp | 110 dp on-key, 65 dp floating |
| Popup distance above key *(shown only while Key popup is on and Popup on key is off)* | Slider | 0–96 dp | 10 dp |
| Popup sideways shift *(same)* | Slider | −64–64 dp | 0 dp |
| Popup shape | Nav row (shape picker) | the twelve key shapes | Rounded |
| Popup corner radius | Slider | 0–40 dp | 12 dp |
| Popup color *(shown only while Key popup is on)* | Color picker | any color, or Automatic | Automatic |
| Popup text color *(same)* | Color picker | any color, or Automatic | Automatic |

**Minimum popup duration** keeps the bubble visible for at least that long after release, so a fast tap doesn't flash it for a single frame. The bubble also has a ceiling of 750 ms from the press, a safety cap that hides a bubble stranded on screen when the release event is dropped under UI lag. That cap used to be a slider; it has no row now, since there was never a reason to move it, and a value set on it before is still honoured. **Popup on key** switches between two looks: the bubble growing up out of the pressed key (the stock-keyboard look), or a compact bubble floating above your fingertip with a gap.

**Popup height** looks like one slider, but it stores a value per bubble style, because the two want very different numbers. An on-key bubble is measured from the bottom of the key it covers, so most of its height goes on climbing back out from under your finger. A floating bubble already starts above the key. Flip **Popup on key** and the slider shows the other style's height. A drag in one mode never resizes the other. A theme can carry its own pair of popup heights too, and when it sets one for the style in use the theme wins; the row then says so and stops responding until the theme lets go. An on-key bubble is never drawn shorter than the key plus its label, whatever this slider or a theme asks for, because a bubble that stops under your finger is a bubble you never see.

**Popup distance above key** and **Popup sideways shift** place the floating bubble, and appear only while **Popup on key** is off. The distance is how far above the pressed key the bubble sits. Raise it when your finger covers the bubble, which is the usual complaint about the floating style, and the keyboard makes the same amount of extra room above the top row so a bubble there is still drawn in full. The sideways shift moves the bubble left (a negative value) or right (a positive value) of the key, which gets it out from behind the hand that holds the phone. It's a screen-space shift rather than a start-relative one, because it's about your hand and not about the script, so an RTL layout doesn't mirror it. Near the edge of the keyboard the bubble stops at the edge instead of leaving the screen.

**Space above the keyboard** is the clear band the keyboard holds above its top row, so a top-row key's bubble is drawn in full over the app. The band isn't counted as keyboard: apps are still laid out down to the keys, and a tap in the band reaches the app. Some phones still move an app in a floating window clear of the band too, which squashes the window against the top of the screen while you type. Turn this off on those phones. The keyboard's window then ends at its top row and a top-row bubble stays inside the keyboard, over its own key, the same as it does under a blurred theme.

**Popup color** and **Popup text color** are the bubble's background and the character inside it. Both read **Automatic** until you pick one, which means the bubble follows the keyboard theme. A color picked here wins over the theme, and the reset control on the row puts it back to Automatic. Unlike the shape and the radius, these two stop at the preview bubble: press-and-hold alternates, the language picker and the panel menus stay on the theme's popup color. A per-key style from a layout still overrides both.

**Popup shape** and **Popup corner radius** reach past the preview bubble. They set the outline of every popup surface the keyboard draws, press-and-hold alternates, the language picker and the panel menus included. The radius applies only to the rounded and cut shapes. The rest size their corners off the popup itself. A theme can override either one.

### Delete keys

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Delete keys" />

| Setting | Type | Default | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swipe to delete | Toggle | On | always |
| Swipe deletes | Words / Characters | Words | Swipe to delete on |
| Show what you will delete | Toggle | On | Swipe to delete on |
| Word delete distance | Slider, 32–120 dp | 72 dp | Swipe deletes = Words |
| Character delete distance | Slider, 8–48 dp | 20 dp | Swipe deletes = Characters |
| Swipe up to clear all | Toggle | Off | always |
| Swipe on forward delete | Toggle | On | always |
| Backspace as a key press on web pages | Toggle | On | always |

A leftward drag on <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> deletes more than the one character a press does, and **Swipe deletes** sets what one step of it takes off. **Word delete distance** is how far you drag before the first word goes, with later words coming off faster on a fixed curve, so that one number tunes the whole gesture; **Character delete distance** is the flat cost of one character, with no curve. **Show what you will delete** makes the swipe select the text and delete it when you lift your finger, so a swipe that went too far can be dragged back.

**Swipe up to clear all** selects everything in the text box when you swipe straight up on <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap>, and deletes it when you lift. Bring your finger back down first and nothing is deleted. See [Swiping up to clear everything](/typing/gestures/#swiping-up-to-clear-everything).

**Swipe on forward delete** gives <KeyCap>⌦</KeyCap> the same drag pointed the other way: rightward, over the text after the cursor. It has its own on/off switch and then borrows every other row above, so the two delete keys cannot end up tuned differently. No built-in layout carries a <KeyCap>⌦</KeyCap> key; add one in the [layout editor](/languages/custom-layouts/). Full writeup: [Gestures](/typing/gestures/#deleting-more-than-a-character-with-backspace). This group moved here from the [Typing](/reference/settings/typing/) screen: what a key does under a swipe is a key-press matter.

**Backspace as a key press on web pages** sends <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> to a text box on a web page, in a browser or inside an app, the way a physical keyboard sends it instead of deleting the text directly. Code editors on a page, such as Violentmonkey's script editor, only delete when they see that key press and put back text deleted any other way. The browser then decides how much one press removes. A language set to delete whole conjuncts keeps the direct delete. Turn it off if backspace misbehaves on a site.

### Alternate keys

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Alternate keys" />

This group is about the press-and-hold popup, not the preview bubble above it. The two are different popups and they size separately.

| Setting | Control | Options / range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alternate key size | Slider | ×0.70–×3.20 | ×1.00 |
| Alternate key spacing | Slider | 0–32 dp | 10 dp |
| Alternate key columns | Stepper | Auto, then 3–11 | Auto |
| Closest to the key first | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Centre on the key | Toggle | On / Off | On |
| Order of the alternates | Drag list | Layout's own / Accents / Capital | Layout's own, accents, capital |
| Choose without lifting | Toggle | On / Off | On |

**Alternate key size** goes twice as far as **Popup font size** does, because the alternates popup grows to hold whatever you give it while the preview bubble has a fixed height to stay inside. If you'd already raised the popup font size before this slider existed, it starts at that value, so nothing shrank on you. From there the two are independent.

**Alternate key spacing** is the room around each character. It's both the gap you see and the target you tap, so raise it if you keep hitting the neighbour. A roomier popup is a wider one, and a wider one wraps onto more rows.

**Alternate key columns** is Auto by default, which fits as many per row as the screen allows and lets the popup's shape follow the width of the characters. Pick a number instead and every row gets that many equal columns, so a key with a dozen alternates reads as a block and each character stays where you last found it. The stepper walks Auto, 3, 4 and up to 11 one press at a time.

The number you pick is a ceiling. If that many columns at your spacing don't fit across the screen, the spacing on either side of each character shrinks first, evenly, so every character stays whole and centred in its column. If even the bare characters don't fit, for example 11 columns at a big **Alternate key size**, the popup uses as many columns as do fit. Either way the popup keeps a small margin from both edges of the screen, so one as wide as the screen sits centred rather than pushed against a side.

This one is the keyboard-wide default. A single key can carry a count of its own, set on **Columns in the popup** in the [layout editor's key sheet](/languages/custom-layouts/#editing-a-key), and where it does, that wins. Keys start at "From settings", so this row is what they all follow until you say otherwise.

**Closest to the key first** decides which end of a multi-row popup the first alternate goes to. Off, the popup fills like lines of text: first alternate top left, and on a tall popup that's the corner furthest from your thumb. On, it fills from the key outward, so the first alternate sits on the row nearest your finger and the overflow climbs away. That's the AOSP behaviour, and it's the shorter reach on keys with long lists. On a one-row popup the two look identical.

**Centre on the key** is on by default. Each row then puts its first alternate in the middle and the rest on both sides of it, nearest first: `1 2 3 4 5 6` lays out as `5 3 1 2 4 6`, with `1` over the key. So a slide left is as useful as a slide right. On a fixed column count, a short row centres on the same column as the full rows, so the column above the key always starts a row. A key near the edge of the screen has no room on one side, so its popup falls back to reading order running away from that edge. Off, every popup runs in reading order, all to one side of the first alternate. **Classic press-and-hold popup** under Accessibility turns centring off too.

With **Choose without lifting** on, a slide steers the highlight from where your finger started, across and up alike. The first alternate is lit a row above your finger when the popup opens, and it stays that far above as you move, so sliding up one row's height reaches the next row without your thumb having to climb onto it and cover it.

**Choose without lifting** is the popup's press-and-hold behaviour, and it's on out of the box. The first alternate is highlighted the moment the popup opens, sliding your finger moves the highlight, and letting go types whatever is highlighted. So a hold and a straight release gives you the first alternate, the way every stock keyboard works. Slide well clear of the popup before you let go and nothing is typed. Turn it off and the popup goes back to waiting: it stays open after you lift, and you tap the one you want. That's two steps, but each one is easier to aim, which is worth having if your hands aren't steady.

### Timing

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Timing" />

| Setting | Control | Options / range | Default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Press and hold delay | Slider | 100–3000 ms | 300 ms |
| Time before a key repeats | Slider | 150–3000 ms | 300 ms |
| Delete repeat interval | Slider | 20–200 ms | 50 ms |
| Holding delete clears words | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Words stop at punctuation | Toggle (shown while a delete takes words) | On / Off | On |
| Words stop at capitals | Toggle (shown while a delete takes words) | On / Off | Off |
| Words stop at numbers | Toggle (shown while a delete takes words) | On / Off | Off |
| Word delete interval | Slider | 60–500 ms (shown while the toggle above is on) | 140 ms |
| Space repeat interval | Slider | 20–200 ms | 100 ms |
| Repeating key interval | Slider | 20–200 ms | 50 ms |
| Hold a flick to repeat it | Toggle | On / Off | Off |
| Caps lock double press | Slider | 150–600 ms | 350 ms |

**Press and hold delay** is how long you hold a key before its alternates popup (accents, digits, symbols) appears. **Time before a key repeats** is the separate wait before a held <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> or <KeyCap>Space</KeyCap> starts repeating. The two used to share one number, so slowing down accent popups for a tremor slowed down backspace with it. The same wait covers any key you turned **Repeat while held** on in the [key layout editor](/reference/settings/keymaps/#a-keys-settings).

The repeat interval is two sliders, because delete and space are held for opposite reasons. **Delete repeat interval** sets how fast a held <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> clears text, and faster is usually better. **Space repeat interval** does the same for a held <KeyCap>Space</KeyCap>. A runaway row of spaces has to be undone afterwards, so that one is worth setting slower, and it ships slower: delete at 50 ms, space at 100 ms.

**Holding delete clears words** changes what the repeat takes: one whole word per tick instead of one letter, on <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> and <KeyCap>⌦</KeyCap> alike, each clearing the way its own key points. A single press is untouched and still deletes one character. Word clearing runs on **Word delete interval** rather than the letter one, and ships slower on purpose: at 50 ms a word a held key empties a paragraph before you can lift your finger. The words come off the way a delete swipe takes them, so the whitespace beside the cursor goes with the word beyond it. Both settings are off and hidden respectively out of the box; the swipe gestures are separate, under [Delete keys](#delete-keys) above. Once a held delete key has started to repeat, it stays a hold: a thumb that rolls sideways on the key no longer turns it into a swipe, which used to stop the delete with your finger still down.

**Words stop at punctuation**, **Words stop at capitals** and **Words stop at numbers** say what one word is, for the held delete key and for a backspace swipe set to words. With punctuation on, as it ships, `wmkeyboard.pages.dev` goes as `dev`, `.`, `pages` and so on, and an underscore counts as punctuation too, while an apostrophe inside a word does not, so `don't` goes whole; off, all of it goes in one step, up to the last space. With capitals on, `GrapheneOS` goes as `OS` and then `Graphene`. With numbers on, `word1` goes as `1` and then `word`. The word cursor tools have their own settings for this, under [Where a word ends](/tools/text-editing/#where-a-word-ends).

**Repeating key interval** is the third of those speeds, and it belongs to the keys you set to repeat yourself, and a row of arrow keys is the usual one. It ships at delete's 50 ms, because an arrow key and a backspace are held for the same reason: keep going until the cursor is where you want it. Delete and space keep their own numbers, so changing this one leaves them alone.

**Caps lock double press** is the window for two <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> taps to lock caps. It only matters on the way into caps lock. A single tap while Shift or Caps Lock is already on always turns it off, whatever the timing.

### Press and hold shortcuts

<PhoneFrame side caption="Press and hold shortcuts: the currency keys field and the hold-letter toggles.">

**Press and hold hints** puts a small corner label on each key, previewing its first press-and-hold character.

**All accents on press and hold** adds the complete accent set to every Latin letter's popup, on top of whatever the layout already lists. Letters only.

**No accents on press and hold** takes the accented forms of each Latin letter (à á â ä and the rest) out of its popup, whether the layout listed them or the setting above added them, so a hold on <KeyCap>e</KeyCap> offers its digit or symbol and nothing else. To drop the accents from a single key instead, edit its alternates in the [layout editor](/reference/settings/keymaps/).

**Top row press and hold** decides what a hold on <KeyCap>q</KeyCap> to <KeyCap>p</KeyCap> types, apart from the number row. The built-in layouts put the digits 1 to 0 there. **Auto**, the default, keeps them while the [number row](/typing/basics/#number-row-and-numerals) is off and drops them while it's on, because the row already has them. **Digits** keeps them with the row on as well. **Symbols** swaps each digit for the symbol above it on a number row, so the top row holds `! @ # $ % ^ & * ( )` and the corner hints show them, with the number row on or off. **None** drops the digits either way, and those keys open with their accents or have no popup. It applies to any layout that puts digits on its letters, QWERTZ and AZERTY included. To put a different character on one key, edit its alternates in the [layout editor](/reference/settings/keymaps/).

**Your language's letters first** is on out of the box. Most letter keys open their popup with a number or symbol (7 on <KeyCap>u</KeyCap>, @ on <KeyCap>a</KeyCap>), and this moves the letters of the language you're typing in front of it. On German, a hold on <KeyCap>u</KeyCap> types ü, <KeyCap>o</KeyCap> gives ö, <KeyCap>a</KeyCap> gives ä and <KeyCap>s</KeyCap> gives ß, and those are the corner hints too. The number stays in the popup, right behind them. Which letters count comes from each language's standard alphabet, so an accent your language doesn't use stays where it was, and English, which has no accented letters, doesn't change at all. It follows the active language, and works with **All accents on press and hold** too. A key whose popup you wrote yourself in the layout editor, or any key of a layout you made or imported, keeps the order you wrote: put the number first there and it stays first. Turn it off to get the number first again everywhere.

**Shifted letter on press and hold** adds the character each key types under Shift to the end of its popup. In Latin scripts that's the capital. In scripts without capitals, like Arabic, Persian or Devanagari, it's the second letter or mark printed on the key, so you can skip Shift for it. Layouts that keep their shifted keys on a page of their own, which is most [Keyman layouts](/languages/keyman/), get them from that page. A key that already lists it keeps the one the layout wrote. It also pairs with the [drag off ?123 or ABC](/typing/gestures/#dragging-from-123-or-abc), where the Shift key is on the layer your finger has left.

**Press and hold ?123 for the number pad** is on out of the box. Hold the symbols key and a full number pad panel opens over the current field. A quick tap still switches to the symbol layer as usual. On a layout whose <KeyCap>?123</KeyCap> has press-and-hold characters of its own, the hold opens those and the number pad joins them as an entry in the popup.

**Press and hold enter for emoji** is off by default. Turn it on and holding <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> opens a popup with an emoji entry first, so with **Choose without lifting** on (the default) a plain hold and release opens the emoji panel. In a field where Enter shows Send, Go or Search, the line break it replaces stays in the same popup, one slide along. A tap still does what Enter always does. The same switch also sits under **Layout & size → Bottom row keys**, beside Comma key opens emoji.

**Currency keys** is stored empty by default. The field shows and edits the built-in set (৳ € £ ¥ ₹ ₿) until you type your own space-separated list. Clear it back to blank and the built-in set comes back.

**Ctrl shortcuts as raw key events** only matters for a Ctrl key on a custom layout, whether you tap it or [drag from it onto another key](/typing/gestures/#dragging-from-a-modifier-key). Off, A/C/V/X route through Android's clipboard actions, which work in web pages and Compose apps too. On, the raw key event goes through instead. A terminal app needs that, since it reads Ctrl+C as interrupt rather than copy.

**Hold shortcuts** is one row of six chips, all on by default: Select all, Copy, Cut, Paste, Undo and Redo. Each one goes into that letter's press-and-hold popup, after the accents the key already has, so holding C still offers `çć` and copy sits at the end of the row. Copy and cut both fall back to select-all when nothing is selected, so one hold copies or cuts the whole field. Turn a chip off and only its own entry goes.

**Action position** sits next to Hold shortcuts and says where the shortcut goes in its key's popup. It starts at **Last**: holding a shortcut letter still opens on its first accent, the same as a letter with no shortcuts, and Copy or Cut sits at the end. **After 1 character** (up to 7) moves the shortcut in among the accents. A plain hold on <KeyCap>C</KeyCap> still types `ç`, the accent the corner hint shows, and Copy is the next entry, a short slide away instead of past every accent. **First** moves it to the front, so a plain hold on <KeyCap>C</KeyCap> runs Copy right away; keep your finger down and slide on to reach `çć` instead of releasing. A place past all of a key's accents puts the shortcut last on that key. A key whose layout places its own actions (**Where the actions go** in a [key's settings](/reference/settings/keymaps/#a-keys-settings)) keeps that place. Anything but Last needs **Choose without lifting**, under Alternate keys, turned on, since a popup that just sits open has nothing to run on release. If you had the old **Action first** switch on, it carries over as First.

**Slide from 🌐 for shortcuts** is off by default. On, pressing 🌐 and sliding onto one of the six shortcut keys runs that key's action when you lift, whether or not its hold shortcut is on. See [Switching languages](/languages/switching/#sliding-from--onto-a-shortcut).

**Shortcut keys** is one row for all six bindings rather than six rows, and it appears whenever at least one of them, or the 🌐 slide, is on. The shipped `a c v x z y` is a QWERTY answer, so a layout without those letters (Bangla, Russian) needs the whole set moved onto keys it draws. The row's subtitle lists the keys currently bound. Tap it to open a dialog with one field per switched-on action, saved in one go.

## Details & edge cases

- **The per-event toggles fold away rather than grey out.** With Key press haptics off, Vibrate on space, Vibrate on delete swipe, Vibrate on key repeat, Follow the system touch feedback setting, Vibrate past the system touch feedback switch and Mute haptics in Do Not Disturb all leave the screen. Click on key repeat follows Key sound instead, not Key press haptics, so it disappears only when Key sound is off.
- **Press and hold delay lives here only.** The Timing slider runs 100–3000 ms and the stored value takes the whole range. Accessibility → Touch shows the current value and links back to this screen.
- **A few rows on this screen aren't reachable from in-app settings search.** Haptic style, Click on key repeat, Follow the system touch feedback setting, Sound style, Key release sound, Time before a key repeats and Shortcut keys are all real, rendered rows here. They're missing from the search index. Everything else is indexed, Key sound, Sound volume, Maximum popup duration, Popup shape, Popup corner radius and Press and hold ?123 among them.
- **Most rows here grow a restore button once you move them, but four never do.** Change a toggle, a slider or the Shortcut keys row and a small restore control appears beside it, putting just that setting back. The two chip rows (Haptic style, Sound style), the Popup shape picker and the Currency keys field are hand-built controls with no restore of their own. Clearing Currency keys back to blank is how you get its built-in set again. See [Putting one setting back](/reference/settings/#putting-one-setting-back).
- **The "Sound & haptics" toolbar tool isn't a separate settings surface.** Its page carries the Key sound switch, plus a nav row into the Haptics and sound page, so a change on either surface writes to the same settings. See [Sound & vibration](/typing/feedback/#a-toolbar-shortcut).
- **Currency keys shows the built-in default even though the stored value is empty.** The field only starts writing your own list once you edit it. Clear it back to blank and you get the same default again, not an empty popup.
- **No build differences.** Haptics, key sounds, popups, timing and press-and-hold shortcuts all work identically in every build.
