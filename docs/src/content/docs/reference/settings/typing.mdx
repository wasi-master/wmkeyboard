The Typing screen is a hub. Seven of its groups are pages of their own (Automatic corrections, Capitals, punctuation and spacing, Suggestions, Smart chips, One-time codes, Gestures, Physical keyboard) and two short groups stay on the hub: Enter key and Volume keys. Gestures is a hub in its own right, with three pages under it: Glide typing, Spacebar, and Flicks and swipes. The Delete keys group used to sit here too; it lives on [Key press](/reference/settings/keypress/#delete-keys) now, with the rest of what a key does under your finger. The tool-shortcuts letter list is one tap deeper still. Most of what's here has its own task-focused guide too, linked from each group below. This page is the field-by-field reference, for checking a default or finding a setting by name.

**Autocorrect** is this group's master switch, and fifteen rows hang off it: the confidence slider, the two rows that tune how hard it tries (Adaptive confidence, Typing rhythm), and everything down to Fix number row slips. Three of those fourteen carry a second condition. How often to offer the undo needs Undo a correction from the strip on. Detection strength needs Detect the language you type on. Fix number row slips shows up only while the [number row](/reference/settings/layout/) is on screen, since it corrects a digit to the letter below it. From **Adapt to app tone** downwards the rows stay put whether autocorrect is on or off.

**Undo a correction from the strip** puts the word you typed on a chip so you can take a correction back after backspace can no longer reach it, and **How often to offer the undo** is the bar a correction has to fall under to earn that chip. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#taking-a-correction-back-later).

**Remember my undos** is one choice of four, and it governs how long the keyboard holds on to a correction you undid, not whether undoing works. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#how-long-an-undo-lasts).

**Stop after I refuse a fix** ends the loop where a correction is deleted, the word retyped, and the correction made again. Once you have undone or deleted a correction at the same place that many times (2 by default), the word you type there is left alone. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#when-you-keep-deleting-the-same-fix).

**Learn from my fixes** remembers the typos you repair by hand, and the slips behind them, and **Learn where my taps land** learns where your finger lands on each key from the words you keep. **Learned corrections** opens the list of both, along with the corrections you undid, and lets you add, change or remove each entry on its own. See [Learning from your own fixes](/smart/autocorrect/#learning-from-your-own-fixes).

**Learned correction gate** replaces the fixed-ratio rule with one fitted judgement over everything the keyboard knows about a candidate, and holds back from rewriting correctly typed new words. See [The learned gate](/smart/autocorrect/#the-learned-gate). **Context model** reads the whole sentence with a small downloaded language model, **Re-check the previous word** offers a fix for a word behind the caret once the words after it show what you meant, and re-reads the last few after a pause, and **Phrase suggestions** offers the next few words as one chip. See [The context model](/smart/suggestions/#the-context-model).

Several of these rows rank candidates rather than correct them, and they sit here because that's where the app puts them. **Context-aware suggestions** reorders the strip using the words before what you're typing, and **Context model**, **Re-check the previous word** and **Phrase suggestions** are the neural model's three rows. **Adapt to app tone** nudges chat-speak up in messaging apps and down in email. **Typing rhythm** lets a fast, sloppy burst make autocorrect fire more eagerly than slow deliberate typing does. Rhythm ships at 0%, so it does nothing until you move the slider.

A navigation row at the bottom opens **Capitals, punctuation and spacing**, the page below: the half of this screen that types for you rather than correcting you, moved out so that this page is autocorrect and nothing else.

Full writeup: [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/) covers the confidence math, what a word is never corrected to, and reverting a correction with backspace. [Mixed-language typing](/smart/mixed-language/) covers Detect the language you type and its three strengths. [Suggestions & prediction](/smart/suggestions/) covers context reranking and app tone.

**Space before French punctuation** types the space French keeps in front of `!`, `?`, `;`, `:` and `»`, and behind `«`, and stops the two rules above from taking it back. **Suggestions** is the master switch for the whole strip: word completions, typo corrections offered as candidates, and next-word prediction. **Punctuation marks** sets which marks the punctuation chips offer, one per character, in the order you want them. Leave it blank and it falls back to the shipped `.,?!'`. **Number of suggestions** splits the strip evenly, so raising it on a narrow screen buys candidates at the cost of reading each one. **More slots for short words** adds up to three slots past that number while the words are short enough to fit them at full size. **Swipe down for more suggestions** opens a grid of up to 24 words over the keys; **Swipe up for more suggestions** opens up to 12 just above the strip, where you keep your finger down, slide onto a word and lift to use it. **More suggestions in a grid** lines either set up in one column per suggestion slot. See [Suggestions](/smart/suggestions/). **Scroll the suggestions** lifts that trade: each word keeps its natural size and the strip scrolls sideways when they overrun it, so five or six candidates read fine on a narrow phone. Short words still fill the strip exactly as before. Turn on **More suggestions arrow** as well and an arrow at the end of the strip opens the rest of the ranked words in a grid over the keys; a swipe down opens the same grid either way. **Shorten long words** picks where a word that still overruns its slot is cut: in the middle, so both its start and its ending stay readable (`Punct…tion`), or at the end (`Punctuat…`). The color the strip paints a correction in lives with the rest of its looks, under [Look & feel / Toolbar](/reference/settings/appearance/).

**Remember a new word after** is how many times you have to type a word nothing recognises before it's saved to your personal dictionary at all. It only counts a use once you've moved on and left the word alone, so a typo you go back and fix never counts. Set it to 1 to save every new word straight away, which is what versions before this did. Words already in a dictionary skip this entirely and are counted the moment you type them.

**Ask before saving new words** replaces that counting with a chip on the suggestion strip reading `Add "yourword"?`. Nothing is saved unless you tap it, and the ✕ stops the keyboard asking about that word again. Turning it on hides the slider above, since the two answer the same question different ways.

**Learn a word after** is a further threshold, applied to words already in your dictionary: how many reinforcements before autocorrect leaves the word alone. At the default of 1 the slider reads "Once". Suggestions are untouched either way: a word under the count can still be offered; it isn't shielded from correction.

All three are covered in full on [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/#words-wait-their-turn).

**Word menu** picks which of the five optional items the press-and-hold menu on a suggested word offers; **Edit** is always on it. **Synonym sources** sets where the menu's **Synonyms** looks, in order: each source is asked only when the ones above it had no synonyms for the word or could not be reached. Drag to reorder, switch a source off to never ask it, or remove it and add it back later with **Add a provider**. **Adjust rank changes** decides which rank controls the word card shows: the learned weight (the personal dictionary's own count), a separate rank adjustment that can move any word up or down, or both. All three settings are covered on [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/#from-the-suggestion-strip).

**Delete edits imported lists** governs what happens when you delete one of your own imported words from the strip. On, the word is removed from that list's file for good. Off, the list stays untouched and the word is blacklisted instead, the same as a word from a downloaded list, which can't be edited.

**Offer corrections it isn't sure about** puts a fix that came close to firing on the strip as a chip rather than dropping it. It never applies anything on its own, and a correction you have already undone once is never offered. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#corrections-it-offers-instead-of-applying).

The three navigation rows at the bottom open their own screens rather than an inline setting. The subtitle on **Suggestion blacklist** starts as "Words to never suggest or autocorrect to" and becomes a running count ("3 words never suggested") once you've added something. All three are documented in full on [Dictionaries & words](/reference/settings/dictionaries/).

### The Autopilot screen

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Suggestions / Autopilot" />

| Setting | Control | Default | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Autopilot | Toggle | On | always (needs Suggestions on to bias toward anything) |
| Strength | Slider 1–20 | 5 | Autopilot on |
| Show the effect | Toggle | Off | Autopilot on |
| Size of the effect | Slider ×1.0–×3.0 | ×1.0 | Show the effect on |
| Outline the touch areas | Toggle | Off | Autopilot on |
| Animate the touch areas | Toggle | Off | Autopilot on |

**Strength** is how far a favoured letter reaches over the key next to it. At 1 only a tap on the exact edge changes hands; at 10 an expected letter claims most of the space between itself and a letter the word list isn't expecting, and from 11 to 20 it pushes further still until the reach limit stops it. No letter ever reaches more than about one key width from its own centre, whatever the slider says, and a press in the middle of a key is never reassigned at any strength.

The other rows make the invisible visible. **Show the effect** draws each expected letter over the board at the size its touch area has grown to, so you can watch the strength setting work while you type. At most three letters are drawn at once, and they're the three the word list expects most, ranked by likelihood rather than by how much room each one won: on a crowded row the likeliest letter can be hemmed in by its neighbours and grow the least of the lot, and it's still the one you're looking for. What each one is drawn with is the ground the bias won it, measured against where the boundary would have sat with nothing favoured at all. On a layout whose keys differ in width that is not the same as the key you see, since the halfway line between a wide key and a narrow one already falls inside the wide one. The keys themselves never move. **Outline the touch areas** draws a thin line around the same edge, which is what your press is judged against. Both are off by default, since the point of the feature is that you don't notice it.

**Animate the touch areas** (off by default) makes that drawing move smoothly: each letter grows or shrinks to its new size instead of jumping on every keystroke, and fades in or out as it gains or loses favour. Only the drawing animates. The areas your presses are judged against change at once, exactly as before. Reduce motion turns it off.

The drawing follows your typing and nothing else. It disappears for the length of a glide, which owns the board while it's being drawn, and moving the caret into a word you typed earlier doesn't bring it back.

**Size of the effect** is how big the drawing gets, and nothing else: your presses land in the same places at every value. ×1.0 draws each area at its true size. Above that it is the growth that multiplies, not the whole key, so a letter that claimed no extra space still draws at the size of its key and only the difference gets larger. Raise it to see a gentle strength setting at a glance, or leave it at ×1.0 for the true picture. The outline follows the same size, so the two always agree.

### The Words on the keys screen

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Suggestions / Words on the keys" />

| Setting | Type | Default | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Words on the keys | Toggle | Off | always |
| Where the words sit | Choice: Above the key / Own lane / Inside the key | Above the key | Words on the keys on |
| How many words | Slider, 3–26 | 6 | Words on the keys on |
| What may appear | Multi-choice: Completions / Corrections / Next words | All three | Words on the keys on |
| While gliding | Choice: Nothing / Other readings / The next word | Nothing | Words on the keys on, glide typing on |
| Flick up to pick | Toggle | On | Words on the keys on |
| Flick sensitivity | Choice: Strict / Balanced / Relaxed | Balanced | Flick up to pick on |
| Tap the word to pick | Toggle | On | Words on the keys on |
| Word size | Slider, ×0.6–×1.6 | ×1.0 | Words on the keys on |

**Words on the keys** floats a predicted word over the key that would type it next: with `hel` typed, `hello` sits over <KeyCap>L</KeyCap> and `help` over <KeyCap>P</KeyCap>. It only adds to the suggestion strip, never replaces it, and ships off. **Where the words sit** chooses the geometry: above the key without growing the keyboard, in a lane of its own with more room and a taller board, or inside the key sharing its small box with the letter. **How many words** trades quiet for coverage. Low values show only the words the keyboard is confident about and often leave the board empty; high values fill nearly every key that can carry one, and a key too narrow for its word stays bare rather than showing it cramped. **What may appear** limits the kinds of guess allowed on the keys: completions continue the word you're typing, corrections fix it and draw in a different color where the match breaks down, and next words appear over their first letters after a space, before you've typed anything.

**While gliding** asks the same question about a stroke, and it has its own answer because the two surfaces want different things. **Nothing**, the default, leaves the keys bare until you lift. **Other readings** floats the alternatives the decoder is still holding, each over the key that reaches it, so you can steer mid-swipe. **The next word** offers what would follow the word you're drawing, over the key it starts with, so a glide typist can flick it the moment the swipe ends. None of the three changes what appears at the lift: a finished glide always puts the next-word offer on the keys. See [Words on the keys](/typing/words-on-the-keys/#during-a-glide).

Two gestures take a floating word. **Flick up to pick** reads a straight, mostly vertical stroke off a key as picking its word rather than starting a glide, and **Flick sensitivity** sets how far off straight up, and how curved, that stroke may still be and count. **Tap the word to pick** writes it with a direct tap on the small floating text, though on the top row only the half hanging over the key can be tapped.

**Hide the corner hint** clears a key's long-press hint while it's carrying a word, since the two sit close enough to read as one thing; a key with nothing to say keeps its hint either way. **Long-press characters too**, off by default, also floats words that need a long press to reach their next letter, at the cost of a key promising a word a plain press won't finish.

While **Two-thumb typing** is on, Glide across the spacebar, Glide over Shift to capitalize, What a pass over Shift capitalizes, Ask when a glide is unclear and its four sub-settings say "Off while two-thumb typing is on" in place of their usual line. Each of them reads one swipe as a whole word, which two-thumb typing does not do. They stay editable, and the values you set take effect again once two-thumb typing is off. The hint and capital flicks work with both thumbs, so their rows carry no such note.

**What a swipe does** is a picker rather than a toggle. It decides what a letter-area swipe means. Its Handwrite option needs the ML Kit handwriting model, so the F-Droid build leaves it out. **Slide to a key** turns a swipe into a way to aim a single key: the key under your finger lights up and shows its preview bubble, the keys you pass over type nothing, and only the key you lift on is typed. Lift outside the keys to type nothing. The glide-only rows (and the trail rows) hide while it is selected. **Extra time for a dot or a cross** is the mirror image of **Delay after typing**. One guards against reading a tap as the start of a glide, the other against reading a tap as a handwriting dot. Only one of the pair is ever on screen, since they hang off different `What a swipe does` choices.

**Ask when a glide is unclear** covers the strokes that have no single right answer. Hold your finger still at the end of one and the closest words appear just above your fingertip. Slide onto one and lift to take it. Lift without choosing and you get the best guess, as before. The stroke is finished the moment the words appear, so sliding to one does not redraw it, and sliding down away from the words and lifting types nothing at all. It matters most on a fixed Bangla layout, where ক and খ share a key, so a word and its aspirated twin are drawn identically. The four rows under it set **When to ask** (how close a call a stroke has to be, from Near ties to Every pause), **Time to hold still** (the pause before the words appear), **Hold longer to always ask** (a confident stroke still asks after twice the pause) and **Words to choose from** (two to five). See [When a swipe is a genuine toss-up](/typing/glide-typing/#when-a-swipe-is-a-genuine-toss-up).

**Trail style** is the one trail control most people need. Thin, Normal and Bold set the trail's width, how long it lingers and how solid it is together, and Off draws no trail at all. The three sliders under it set each value on its own and sit behind the group's **More settings** row at the Simple and Standard depths; the style reads back as whichever preset the width is nearest to, with Off whenever the opacity is zero. A theme that pins the trail's width or opacity hides the style row, since the sliders say which theme is holding them.

### The Tool shortcuts list screen

Tapping **Tool shortcuts list** opens a dedicated screen (route `hwshortcuts`) with no reference page of its own, so it's documented here instead. It has two groups:

A chord apps commonly claim for themselves (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+S, Ctrl+Tab) is still accepted, with a warning rather than a block.

Type a letter or a digit there, since the field works with no keyboard plugged in. If that character is already taken, the dialog says which tool loses it and the button reads **Move the letter**.

## Details & edge cases

- **"Space after a suggestion" sits in the wrong-sounding group on purpose.** It reads like a Suggestions setting, but the app itself files it under Capitals, punctuation and spacing, alongside the other space-insertion rules (double-space period/tab, auto-space after punctuation) it shares logic with.
- **Structured and secure fields gate autocorrect, suggestions and the autopilot bias together. Smart chips use a related but separate check.** Those first three share one check keyed on field kind: plain text only. Password fields, email fields and every keypad field (number, phone, date, time, date-time) lose all three, whatever each toggle says. A URL field is the one exception, and only for suggestions: with **Suggestions in every field** on, the keyboard composes the words you type in a browser's address bar and completes them from the strip, while autocorrect and the autopilot bias stay off there, so an address is never second-guessed. Smart chips instead follow whether the app has asked to hide the suggestion strip. So with **Suggestions in every field** on (the default), a calc or currency chip can still appear in an email field where autocorrect and suggestions stay off. Number, phone, date and password fields block chips either way.
- **Three separate settings each add a space, and none of them covers the others.** "Automatic space after punctuation" spaces what follows a mark. "Space after a suggestion" spaces a word tapped from the strip. "Space after a glided word" spaces a word a swipe committed. All three treat that space as the keyboard's to take back, so punctuation typed next removes it and a space press is spent on it. Each has its own switch, and turning one off leaves the other two alone.
- **The two swipe-down settings are independent**: "Swipe down to hide the keyboard" here under Gestures › Spacebar, and "Swipe down to hide" for the toolbar under [Appearance](/reference/settings/appearance/). Turning one on has no effect on the other.
- **The Handwrite option of What a swipe does is not in the F-Droid build**, since it needs the ML Kit handwriting model. There the picker offers Type words and Slide to a key.
- **Slide to a key leaves other drags alone.** A drag from a key with flick directions, from the possessive key, from the space bar, the delete key, Shift, a modifier or `?123` keeps its own gesture. A slide only starts once the finger is over a different key, so a wobble inside one key is still a tap, and a held key whose alternates popup has opened keeps choosing from that popup. It replaces glide typing, the octopus flick and the short hint and capital flicks on letter keys while it is selected.
- **"Apostrophe in a glide" takes one key, and picking the spacebar switches off "Glide across the spacebar".** One crossing can't mean both "the word ends here" and "put an apostrophe here", so while the spacebar is the apostrophe key the multi-word split stands down. The other three choices (comma, full stop, a real apostrophe key if your layout has one) leave it alone. Picking a key your current layout doesn't have does nothing at all rather than falling back to some other key. The chosen key still types its own character when you press it, and going through it in a glide is always optional: the same word drawn straight still decodes. This setting is only about the apostrophe. Every other piece of in-word punctuation (hyphen, dot, at sign, underscore, ampersand, plus, slash) needs no key and no setting: draw the letters, skip the punctuation, and a word your dictionary spells "f-droid" comes back spelled that way. See [Glide typing](/typing/glide-typing/).
- **"Swipe to add 's" is a gesture of its own, not part of glide typing.** It takes its own key (comma, full stop, or a real apostrophe key if the layout has one), works with glide typing on or off, and extends whatever word sits behind the cursor: one you glided, one you tapped out, or one you're still typing. Swiping to `t`, `d`, `m`, `l`, `r` or `v` adds `'t`, `'d`, `'m`, `'ll`, `'re` or `'ve` instead (`'t` only after a word ending in n), and a line from the key to your finger shows the swipe while you draw it. Only the shape is strict: a short stroke from that key to the letter with a path no more than 1.6× the straight line between the two keys, which is what keeps a real word glided between the same two keys from being read as a possessive. A space after the word moves behind the `'s`, so the field reads "developer's " and never "developer 's". Straight after a glide, one backspace still takes the whole word back. The possessive joins your personal dictionary, so next time you can glide "developer's" in one stroke. It adds English endings to English words only: on an English layout, or on another language's layout with English among its secondary languages (Banglish on Avro, say), where it takes a word in plain Latin letters and leaves a Bengali word alone.
- **Every screen on this page has a deep link.** `wmkeyboard://settings/typing`, `wmkeyboard://settings/typing/corrections`, `wmkeyboard://settings/typing/gestures/glide`, `wmkeyboard://settings/hwshortcuts` and the rest. A link or a favourite that still names a row on the screen it used to sit on, such as a glide row on `typing/gestures`, opens the page the row is on now. A single row has one too: `wmkeyboard://setting/typing_autocorrect_title` opens Automatic corrections and flashes the Autocorrect switch. See [Deep links](/reference/deep-links/) for the full table.
- **Autopilot needs Suggestions on**, not Autocorrect. It biases boundary taps toward whichever letter the suggestion engine's own prediction favours, so with Suggestions off there's nothing for it to bias toward.
- **Three rows sit in Automatic corrections without being corrections.** Context-aware suggestions, Adapt to app tone and Typing rhythm all reorder or reweight candidates. Two of them still hang off the Autocorrect switch, Context-aware suggestions and Typing rhythm, so turn autocorrect off and the reranking goes with it. Adapt to app tone stays.
- **Every row with a control carries the [per-setting reset](/reference/settings/#putting-one-setting-back)**, sliders, pickers and the two text fields included. A restore button next to a control means that row no longer matches the shipped default listed above. The navigation rows (Personal dictionary, Custom dictionaries, Suggestion blacklist, Tool shortcuts list) have nothing to reset, since they only open another screen.
