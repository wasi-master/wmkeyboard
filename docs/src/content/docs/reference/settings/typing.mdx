The Typing screen is the largest single screen in Settings. Nine groups cover everything from autocorrect confidence to physical-keyboard shortcuts, plus one sub-screen a tap deeper (the tool-shortcuts letter list). Most of what's here has its own task-focused guide too, linked from each group below. This page is the field-by-field reference, for checking a default or finding a setting by name.

| Double space for a full stop | Toggle | On | always |
| Double space for a tab | Toggle | Off | always |
| Double space speed | Slider, 200–800 ms | 400 ms | either double-space rule on |
| Automatic space after punctuation | Toggle | Off | always |
| Space after a suggestion | Toggle | On | always |
| Wrap the selection with brackets | Toggle | On | always |
| Shift changes the case of the selection | Toggle | On | always |

**Autocorrect** is this group's master switch, and eleven rows hang off it: the confidence slider, the two rows that tune how hard it tries (Adaptive confidence, Typing rhythm), and everything down to Fix number row slips. Two of those eleven carry a second condition. Detection strength needs Detect the language you type on. Fix number row slips shows up only while the [number row](/reference/settings/layout/) is actually on screen, since it corrects a digit to the letter below it. From **Adapt to app tone** downwards the rows stay put whether autocorrect is on or off.

**Double space for a tab** takes priority over **Double space for a full stop** when both are on, and the **Double space speed** slider they share only appears once one of them is. **Space after a suggestion** lives in this group even though it governs the suggestion strip, not autocorrect. That's where the app's own settings screen files it, so that's where it is here.

Three of these rows rank candidates rather than correct them, and they sit here because that's where the app puts them. **Context-aware suggestions** reorders the strip using the words before what you're typing. **Adapt to app tone** nudges chat-speak up in messaging apps and down in email. **Typing rhythm** lets a fast, sloppy burst make autocorrect fire more eagerly than slow deliberate typing does. Rhythm ships at 0%, so it does nothing until you move the slider.

[Mixed-language typing](/smart/mixed-language/) covers Detect the language you type and its three strengths. [Suggestions & prediction](/smart/suggestions/) covers auto-space-after-suggestion, context reranking and app tone, and [Gestures](/typing/gestures/) and [Text editing](/tools/text-editing/) cover selection-wrap and shift-recase.

| Remember a new word after | Slider, 1–10 ("Straight away" at 1) | 3 | Ask before saving new words off |
| Suggestions in every field | Toggle | On | always |
| Suggestion strip always visible | Toggle | Off | always |
| Best suggestion in the middle | Toggle | On | always |
| Suggest contact names | Toggle | Off | always (requests Contacts permission) |
| Suggest contact emails | Toggle | Off | always (requests Contacts permission) |
| Contact emails in email fields too | Toggle | On | Suggest contact emails on |
| Suggest app names | Toggle | Off | always |
| Inline emoji search | Toggle | On | always |
| Password manager suggestions | Toggle | On | Android 11+ only |
| Smart replies | Toggle | On | Android 11+ only |
| Smart detection of key hits | Toggle | On | always (needs Suggestions on to bias toward anything) |
| Personal dictionary | Nav row | n/a | always (opens the [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/) screen) |
| Custom dictionaries | Nav row | n/a | always (opens the Custom dictionaries screen) |
| Suggestion blacklist | Nav row | n/a | always (opens the [Suggestion blacklist](/smart/blacklist/) screen) |

**Suggestions** is the master switch for the whole strip: word completions, typo corrections offered as candidates, and next-word prediction. **Punctuation marks** sets which marks the punctuation chips offer, one per character, in the order you want them. Leave it blank and it falls back to the shipped `.,?!'`. **Number of suggestions** splits the strip evenly, so raising it on a narrow screen buys candidates at the cost of reading each one. **Scroll the suggestions** lifts that trade: each word keeps its natural size and the strip scrolls sideways when they overrun it, so five or six candidates read fine on a narrow phone. Short words still fill the strip exactly as before.

**Remember a new word after** is how many times you have to type a word *nothing recognises* before it's saved to your personal dictionary at all. It only counts a use once you've moved on and left the word alone, so a typo you go back and fix never counts. Set it to 1 to save every new word straight away, which is what versions before this did. Words already in a dictionary skip this entirely and are counted the moment you type them.

**Ask before saving new words** replaces that counting with a chip on the suggestion strip reading `Add "yourword"?`. Nothing is saved unless you tap it, and the ✕ stops the keyboard asking about that word again. Turning it on hides the slider above, since the two answer the same question different ways.

**Learn a word after** is a further threshold, applied to words already in your dictionary: how many reinforcements before autocorrect leaves the word alone. At the default of 1 the slider reads "Once". Suggestions are untouched either way: a word under the count can still be offered, it just isn't shielded from correction.

All three are covered in full on [Personal dictionary](/smart/personal-dictionary/#new-words-wait-their-turn).

**Offer corrections it isn't sure about** puts a fix that came close to firing on the strip as a chip rather than dropping it. It never applies anything on its own, and a correction you have already undone once is never offered. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#corrections-it-offers-instead-of-applying).

The three navigation rows at the bottom open their own screens rather than an inline setting. The subtitle on **Suggestion blacklist** starts as "Words to never suggest or autocorrect to" and becomes a running count ("3 words never suggested") once you've added something. All three are documented in full on [Dictionaries & words](/reference/settings/dictionaries/).

## One-time codes

<SettingsPath path="Typing / One-time codes" />

| Setting | Type | Default | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Codes from notifications | Toggle | Off | always |
| Allow notification access | Nav row | n/a | Codes from notifications on and access not granted yet |
| Only in number fields | Toggle | On | Codes from notifications on |
| Offer a code for | Slider, 1–10 min | 3 min | Codes from notifications on |
| Clear the notification | Toggle | Off | Codes from notifications on |
| Type codes one character at a time | Toggle | On | always |

**Codes from notifications** is the only row here that needs a grant. Turn it on and you get a disclosure, then the system's notification-listener screen. The **Allow notification access** row is the way back to that screen, and it disappears once access is granted. Turn the toggle off and the keyboard stops reading notifications at once, dropping any code it was holding.

**Type codes one character at a time** sits outside the enabled block on purpose. It governs how *every* code is typed, including one tapped from a clipboard chip, which needs no notification access at all.

**Handwrite with swipes** is a two-option picker rather than a toggle. It decides what a letter-area swipe *means*, and it only appears in the full edition, which is the one that bundles the ML Kit handwriting model. **Extra time for a dot or a cross** is the mirror image of **Delay after typing**. One guards against reading a tap as the start of a glide, the other against reading a tap as a handwriting dot. Only one of the pair is ever on screen, since they hang off opposite `Handwrite with swipes` choices.

**Ask when a glide is unclear** covers the strokes that have no single right answer. Hold your finger still at the end of one and the closest words appear under your fingertip. Slide onto one and lift to take it. Lift without choosing and you get the best guess, as before. It matters most on a fixed Bangla layout, where ক and খ share a key, so a word and its aspirated twin are drawn identically.

## Backspace

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Backspace" />

| Setting | Type | Default | Shown when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Swipe to delete words | Toggle | On | always |
| Word delete distance | Slider, 32–120 dp | 72 dp | Swipe to delete words on |

A leftward drag on <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> deletes a word at a time instead of one character per press, and **Word delete distance** is how far you drag before the first word goes. Later words come off faster on a fixed curve, so that one number tunes the whole gesture. Full writeup: [Gestures](/typing/gestures/#deleting-whole-words-with-backspace).

## Enter key

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Enter key" />

| Setting | Type | Default |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shift and Enter type a new line | Toggle | On |

With this on, holding <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> (or pressing it first on a physical keyboard) makes <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> insert a line break instead of triggering the field's Send/Go/Search action. Only a shift you pressed yourself counts: auto-capitalize's own shift doesn't arm it, and caps lock is ignored.

Turning it off doesn't take line breaks away. Press and hold <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> in any field where it shows the app's action, and the popup offers one.

**Process the physical keys** only governs typed characters. Turning it off stops physical keys from running through autocorrect and suggestions, but Tool shortcuts, panel navigation, and Esc keep working regardless, since none of them touch typed text.

Three rows decide how much the keyboard takes from your apps. Check them first if a shortcut you rely on stops working. **Ctrl + number opens a toolbar tool** claims Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+0 while a text field has focus, which is exactly what browsers and editors use to switch tabs. **Ctrl+Space changes the language** claims the chord code editors use for completions. **macOS shortcuts** is off by default and only makes sense on a Mac keyboard, because on a PC one the Meta key is the Search/Windows key and belongs to the system.

**Number keys select suggestions** ships on **Alt + number**, so a digit picks a strip candidate with no shortcut key first. **Show the suggestion numbers** only appears in that mode. It puts the key under each suggestion for as long as a physical keyboard is attached, and on "After the shortcut key" the numbers already show at the moment they work. **Time to press a key** is how long the badges stay up after the shortcut key. Nothing else happens until they clear or you press one.

### The Tool shortcuts list screen

Tapping **Tool shortcuts list** opens a dedicated screen (route `hwshortcuts`) with no reference page of its own, so it's documented here instead. It has two groups:

- **Shortcut key**: one nav row showing the current trigger, a double-tapped modifier or a captured chord, defaulting to **Double-tap Ctrl**. Tap it to open a capture dialog and record a different double-tap, or an arbitrary chord live from an attached keyboard. A chord apps commonly claim for themselves (Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+S, Ctrl+Tab) is still accepted, with a warning rather than a block.
- **Tools**: one row per supported tool, bound ones first sorted by letter, then unbound ones. Each row shows the tool's icon, its name, and its assigned letter, or "None" where nothing is bound. A tool turned off under [Tools](/reference/settings/tools/) shows a "This tool is off in Tools" caption instead. Tap a row to open a dialog with a one-character field. Type a letter or a digit there, since the field works with no keyboard plugged in. If that character is already taken, the dialog says which tool loses it and the button reads **Move the letter**.

A **Reset to defaults** button at the bottom restores every letter and the shortcut key itself, behind a confirmation dialog.

<PhoneFrame caption="The Tool shortcuts list screen: every tool with its assigned letter.">
  ![The Tool shortcuts list screen: every tool with its assigned letter.](@assets/screens/reference/settings/typing-hwshortcuts-screen.webp)

<KeyCap>T</KeyCap> (toolbox) and <KeyCap>?</KeyCap> (cheat sheet) are reserved and can never be bound to a tool. Every one of the other 25 letters ships bound to something, close to each tool's own initial. This page only covers the settings screen. The full binding table lives on [Hardware keyboards](/typing/hardware-keyboard/#opening-tools-with-the-leader-key), and the printable cheat sheet on [Hardware shortcuts](/reference/shortcuts/).

## Details & edge cases

- **"Space after a suggestion" sits in the wrong-sounding group on purpose.** It reads like a Suggestions setting, but the app itself files it under Automatic corrections, alongside the other space-insertion rules (double-space period/tab, auto-space after punctuation) it shares logic with.
- **Structured and secure fields gate autocorrect, suggestions and the smart key-hit bias together. Smart chips use a related but separate check.** Those first three share one check keyed on field kind: plain text only. Password fields, the two structured letter fields (email, URL) and every keypad field (number, phone, date, time, date-time) lose all three, whatever each toggle says. Smart chips instead follow whether the app has asked to hide the suggestion strip. So with **Suggestions in every field** on (the default), a calc or currency chip can still appear in an email or URL field where autocorrect and suggestions stay off. Number, phone, date and password fields block chips either way.
- **Three separate settings each add a space, and none of them covers the others.** "Automatic space after punctuation" spaces what follows a mark. "Space after a suggestion" spaces a word tapped from the strip. "Space after a glided word" spaces a word a swipe committed. All three treat that space as the keyboard's to take back, so punctuation typed next removes it and a space press is spent on it. Each has its own switch, and turning one off leaves the other two alone.
- **The two swipe-down settings are genuinely independent**: "Swipe down to hide the keyboard" here under Gestures, and "Swipe down to hide" for the toolbar under [Appearance](/reference/settings/appearance/). Turning one on has no effect on the other.
- **Handwrite with swipes only exists in the full edition**, since it needs the bundled ML Kit handwriting model. On lite, the Gestures group goes straight from Glide typing to Glide across the spacebar.
- **"Apostrophe in a glide" takes one key, and picking the spacebar switches off "Glide across the spacebar".** One crossing can't mean both "the word ends here" and "put an apostrophe here", so while the spacebar is the apostrophe key the multi-word split stands down. The other three choices (comma, full stop, a real apostrophe key if your layout has one) leave it alone. Picking a key your current layout doesn't have does nothing at all rather than falling back to some other key. The chosen key still types its own character when you press it, and going through it in a glide is always optional: the same word drawn straight still decodes.
- **"Swipe to add 's" only fires on the stroke straight after a glide, and only on a short straight one.** It needs three things at once: a word the last glide committed, a stroke that starts on the apostrophe key and ends on `s`, and a path no more than 1.6× the straight line between those two keys. That last one is what keeps a real word drawn between the same two keys from being read as a possessive. Type anything, tap a suggestion or move the cursor in between and the offer is gone, because the word it would attach to is no longer the last thing the keyboard put in the field. The possessive joins your personal dictionary, so the next time you can glide "developer's" in one stroke.
- **The Tool shortcuts list route isn't reachable by deep link.** Unlike Typing itself, it doesn't appear in the app's `wmkeyboard://settings/` allowlist, so getting there means opening Typing first and tapping through.
- **Smart detection of key hits needs Suggestions on**, not Autocorrect. It biases boundary taps toward whichever letter the suggestion engine's own prediction favours, so with Suggestions off there's nothing for it to bias toward.
- **Three rows sit in Automatic corrections without being corrections.** Context-aware suggestions, Adapt to app tone and Typing rhythm all reorder or reweight candidates. Two of them still hang off the Autocorrect switch, Context-aware suggestions and Typing rhythm, so turn autocorrect off and the reranking goes with it. Adapt to app tone stays.
- **Every row with a control carries the [per-setting reset](/reference/settings/#putting-one-setting-back)**, sliders, pickers and the two text fields included. A restore button next to a control means that row no longer matches the shipped default listed above. The navigation rows (Personal dictionary, Custom dictionaries, Suggestion blacklist, Tool shortcuts list) have nothing to reset, since they only open another screen.
