Power saving drops the parts of WM Keyboard that cost the most battery: vibration, key sounds, animations, the glide trail, background network fetches. It goes on with one tap. It never turns on by itself unless you ask it to follow Android's battery saver or a low battery. It's a view laid over your settings rather than a rewrite of them. Turn it off and everything comes back exactly as you had it, because nothing was ever changed.

## Turning it on

The quickest way is the **Power saving** toolbar tile. One tap flips it on or off, and a toast confirms which ("Power saving is on", "Power saving is off"). Nothing ever pins it to the toolbar for you, so drag it up from the toolbox yourself, or reach it from **Tools** in settings the way you'd reach any other tool. On a physical keyboard, double-tap <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap> and press <KeyCap>B</KeyCap> to do the same without touching the screen. See [Hardware keyboards](/typing/hardware-keyboard/) for the rest of the leader-key shortcuts.

However you flip it, the manual switch is sticky. It survives the keyboard being torn down and rebuilt between fields, and neither a full battery nor the charger going in turns it back off. Once you've turned it on by hand, it stays on until you turn it off by hand.

## Switching on by itself

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Power saving" />

<PhoneFrame side caption="The Power saving group: the switch, the automatic trigger picker, and the battery slider.">
  <Shot id="typing/power-saving-settings-triggers" alt="The Power saving group: the switch, the automatic trigger picker, and the battery slider." />
</PhoneFrame>

Beyond the manual switch, **Turn on by itself** decides whether power saving also arms automatically:

- **Only when I turn it on**, the default. The manual switch is the only way in, so the keyboard never changes how it feels on its own.
- **Android's battery saver is on.** Power saving follows the system's own Battery Saver toggle in lockstep.
- **The battery is low.** It arms once the battery drops to or below **Low battery is**, a slider from 5% to 50% (default 20%). The slider only appears on this trigger and the next.
- **Battery saver or a low battery.** Either condition arms it.

Two things temper the automatic triggers. **Off while charging** (on by default) suppresses them entirely while the charger is in, since there's nothing to save with power flowing in, and a phone sitting at 100% on the charger counts as charging too. An unread battery level, before the keyboard has taken a reading, is always treated as "not low", so it can never falsely trigger on startup. Neither of these touches the manual switch: turning it on by hand means it, charger or no charger.

:::note
This is a one-way follow. Turning on the keyboard's own power saving never touches Android's system Battery Saver setting: only the reverse.
:::

The keyboard watches a handful of coarse system broadcasts (battery low, battery okay, charger connected and disconnected, battery-saver mode changed) rather than the noisy per-percent battery broadcast. Toggling the charger or the system's battery saver mid-sentence therefore takes effect immediately. The exact battery percentage is a different matter: it's re-read only each time the keyboard comes back on screen. On **The battery is low** trigger, crossing your threshold while the keyboard is already open doesn't switch power saving on until you switch fields or reopen it.

## What gets dropped

<PhoneFrame side caption="The three drop cards: effects, typing helpers and background work.">
  <Shot id="typing/power-saving-what-to-drop" alt="The What to drop group: seventeen independent switches." />
</PhoneFrame>

Power saving is seventeen independent switches plus one drop with no switch of its own, and a **What to drop** row above them that sets fifteen of them as a bundle: **Light** (vibration, sounds, animations, moving themes, the glide trail, glass blur and the background watchers), **Balanced** (Light plus background fetches and the on-device models; the shipped default) or **Aggressive** (Balanced plus glide typing, emoji suggestions, smart chips, the floating button and loaded models). Change any of those fifteen by hand and the row reads **Custom**. **Dark theme** and **Word suggestions** are in no bundle: picking one leaves them as you set them, and they don't make the row read Custom. Each switch defaults to whatever gives up the least for the most battery back.

| Switch | Default | What it drops |
|---|---|---|
| Key vibration | On | The vibration motor on every keystroke (the single biggest per-key power draw). |
| Key sounds | On | The key click sound. See [Sound & vibration](/typing/feedback/). |
| Animations | On | Every transition and motion effect (the same flag [Reduce motion](/accessibility/overview/#reducing-motion) uses), so it can end up on even if you never touched that toggle. |
| Glide trail | On | Only the comet trail drawn behind a swipe. The swipe itself keeps decoding: see [Glide typing](/typing/glide-typing/). |
| Glass blur | On | A [glass theme](/themes/glass/)'s blur of the app behind the keyboard and under its popups, which the system redraws every frame. The glass keeps its rim, grain and wallpaper or image backdrop, and the board stands on that backdrop instead. |
| Dark theme | Off | Nothing is dropped: the keyboard wears Pitch black, a plain built-in theme of near-black keys on true black, which costs the least on an OLED screen and has no image, blur or motion to draw. Your own theme and your light and dark pair stay set and come back when saving ends. A [Keyboard Mode](/typing/modes/) with a theme of its own still wears that theme. Off by default because it changes how the keyboard looks. |
| Moving themes | On | The parts of a theme that move: an animated background image shows as a still picture, a [moving background](/themes/editor/#moving-background) holds a single frame, and key lighting, the glow under your finger and press particles go. Colours, pictures and key finishes stay. Separate from Animations, so panels can keep their transitions while the theme stands still. |
| Glide typing | Off | Swipe-to-type entirely. Left off by default: decoding a swipe is the most expensive thing the keyboard does, but it's also why many people installed it, so only the trail is dropped unless you opt into this too. |
| Emoji suggestions | Off | Scanning what you type for emoji to suggest. Off by default: it's a dictionary look-up on a word you've already typed, and a strip that stops offering emoji reads as broken rather than as saving power. |
| Word suggestions | Off | The words the strip offers to complete or predict, as if the Suggestions switch in Typing were off. Off by default for the same reason as emoji suggestions: a strip that goes blank reads as broken. |
| Smart chips | Off | The calc, currency, unit and keyword chip family: see [Smart chips](/smart/chips/). Off by default for the same reason as emoji suggestions: a few pattern matches on the current word aren't where a battery goes. Clipboard's own entity detection (pulling codes, numbers and links out of what you copy) isn't affected either way. |
| Background network | On | Unrequested fetches only: link previews for copied URLs and scanned QR codes, and the dictionary's look-up-on-selection. Tools you open by hand (translate, search, GIFs) still work. |
| Screenshot watching | On | Watching the screenshot folder for new images to offer in the clipboard. |
| On-device models | On | Whisper dictation falls back to the system recognizer, and the letter-swipe action falls back to gesture typing if it was set to handwriting. See [Offline voice](/tools/whisper/) and [Handwriting](/tools/handwriting/). |
| Media tool pin | On | Putting [Media controls](/tools/media/) on the toolbar by itself while music plays. The pin itself costs nothing to draw, but keeping it right means watching the media session for as long as the keyboard is up. The tool stays reachable from the toolbox. |
| Floating button | Off | Hides the [floating button](/reference/settings/floating-button/), a small window Android draws over every app. It comes back where you left it when saving ends. Off by default because some people open the keyboard with it; with this off the button stays, and Animations stops its quick menu from animating. |
| Loaded models | Off | Waiting 2 minutes after the keyboard hides before it unloads its models (the language model, voice typing, the translator, text scanning and the rest). With this on, it unloads them after 15 seconds instead. Off by default because a loaded model costs memory more than battery, and loading it again for the next field is what uses battery. [Resources](/reference/settings/about/#seeing-what-the-keyboard-holds-in-memory) shows what is loaded and unloads any of it by hand. |

Power saving also always turns off [Words on the keys](/typing/words-on-the-keys/), the predicted words that float over the keys. Unlike the switches above, this one has no settings row of its own. Whenever power saving is in force, the floating words are off, full stop.

A few things are deliberately left alone. The compass and the bubble level read their sensors only while their panel is open, so they stop the moment you close them. The clipboard listener is what fills your clipboard history, so stopping it would lose what you copy rather than save anything.

To see where the keyboard's memory and processor time actually go, open [Resources](/reference/settings/about/#seeing-what-the-keyboard-holds-in-memory) in About.

Power saving only does anything *useful* if at least one of these is on. Turn every switch off and the tile's toast admits it plainly: "Power saving is on. No feature is set to stop yet."

If what you're protecting is a data allowance rather than a battery, [Data saver](/privacy/data-saver/) is the same idea pointed at the network, with a per-feature ask-first answer power saving has no use for. Both can be on at once, and either one holding a fetch back is enough.

## When the phone is busy

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Power saving" />

**Adapt to the phone's load** is on by default, and it works whether or not power saving is. The keyboard keeps an eye on four things that cost it next to nothing to read: how many of its own frames arrive late, how long the suggestion strip takes to read after a key, the phone's temperature (Android 10 and later), and whether the phone is short of memory. From those it settles on one of three states.

- **Calm.** Everything runs as you set it.
- **Busy.** The keyboard saves work you can't see. A moving background draws 30 frames a second instead of 60, and an animated gradient 15 instead of 30. The suggestion strip, the [smart punctuation key](/smart/autocorrect/#the-smart-punctuation-key) and glide previews wait a little longer for a burst of typing to end, so a run of keys costs one read instead of several. A phone with little memory or a slow processor stays at this level all the time, even when nothing else is running.
- **Struggling.** On top of that, a moving theme holds still, the system blur behind a [glass theme](/themes/glass/) stops, [Words on the keys](/typing/words-on-the-keys/) goes, and panel animations are cut.

The keyboard moves up a state after a couple of seconds of strain, or at once when the phone is hot or the system asks for memory. It moves back down one state at a time, and only after half a minute of calm. When stilling a theme was what fixed the frames, the theme moving again would bring the stutter straight back, so each quick return doubles the calm it waits for next time, up to ten minutes. Like power saving, this is a view over your settings: nothing you chose is changed, and every feature comes back by itself.

Turn the switch off if you would rather the keyboard always look and behave the same, whatever the phone is doing.

Some of this applies to everyone, all the time. A [moving background](/themes/editor/#moving-background) never draws more than 60 frames a second, and an animated gradient never more than 30, however fast the screen refreshes. On a 120 Hz screen that halves or quarters what a moving theme costs, and nobody can tell the difference.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Power saving" />

The **Power saving** group holds the controls above:

| Setting | Default |
|---|---|
| Power saving now | Off |
| What to drop | Balanced |
| Turn on by itself | Only when I turn it on |
| Low battery is | 20% (5%–50% range) |
| Off while charging | On |

**Adapt to the phone's load** (on) sits in its own **When the phone is busy** card below it.

Below it the seventeen switches sit in three cards, shown at the Everything settings depth: **Effects to drop** (Key vibration, Key sounds, Animations, Glide trail, Glass blur, Moving themes, Dark theme), **Typing helpers to drop** (Glide typing, Emoji suggestions, Smart chips, Word suggestions) and **Background work to drop** (Background network, Screenshot watching, On-device models, Media tool pin, Floating button, Loaded models). The key popup and the typing statistics are no longer droppable: the popup is cheap to draw and the counters are a few integer bumps per keystroke, so neither saved anything you could measure.

## Details & edge cases

- **Nothing is ever overwritten.** The reduced settings are computed on the way out, the moment power saving applies. The stored values never change, so turning it off, the charger going in, or the battery recovering all bring every feature back exactly as you set it.
- **Turning off the manual switch doesn't always turn saving off.** If an automatic trigger, low battery say, is still holding it on, the toast reads "Power saving stays on. The battery trigger is on." instead of "Power saving is off".
- **The toolbar tile lights up while power saving is actually in force**, the way the Incognito tile does. It follows the fully resolved state rather than just the manual switch, so it's lit while an automatic trigger is holding things on too.
- **Never pinned for you.** Power saving is in none of the shipped toolbars, phone or tablet, so it's always yours to pin. Whether it arrives *switched on* depends on setup: the wizard's "Show me everything" answer turns it on, "A good middle" and "Keep it simple" don't, and it's also topped up into a shorter set when something else in that set can't run on your device (the F-Droid build's missing handwriting and text scanning, or a phone with no Google services).
- **Works before you unlock your device once after a restart**, and behaves identically in every build.
