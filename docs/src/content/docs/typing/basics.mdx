Every key does more than the letter printed on it. A tap commits that letter. A hold opens accents, digits, symbols, or an editing command on six letters you can arm yourself. This page covers what one key does under your finger, before the gestures that travel across several.

## Tapping and long-pressing a key

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Key popup" />

While you hold a key, its character floats in a bubble above your finger. That bubble is the **key popup**, and it lets you confirm what you hit before you let go. It's on by default, and by default it grows upward out of the pressed key instead of hovering above it with a gap (the **Popup on key** toggle). Even a fast tap leaves it up for at least 140&nbsp;ms after you release, so a quick press still flashes something readable. On number, phone and date/time keypads it stays hidden by default, a numeric PIN field included. Echoing each digit large enough to read over your shoulder is not much of a favor. The **Popup on number pads** toggle brings it back on those keypads if you want it there.

<GestureDemo type="long-press" caption="Hold a key past the long-press delay to open its alternates." />

Hold past the **long-press delay** (300&nbsp;ms by default) and the key's alternates pop up, if it has any. `Q`–`P` lead with the digits 1–0. `A`–`L` and `Z`–`M` lead with a symbol (`@`, `#`, `$`, `&`…). Some letters carry accented forms after that first alternate: `e`, `u`, `i`, `o` after their digit (`e` → è é ê ë, `u` → ù ú û ü, and so on), and `a`, `s`, `z`, `c`, `n` after their symbol. The first alternate also prints as a small corner hint on the key itself, so you can see what a hold will lead with before you commit to it. That's the **Press and hold hints** toggle, on by default.

A popup with more alternates than fit across the screen wraps onto a second row and a third, so nothing ever sits off the edge where you can't reach it. That's the case you meet with "All accents on press and hold" turned on, or with a raised popup text size, or on a key you gave a long list of your own.

The popup isn't only for characters. A key in a [custom layout](/languages/custom-layouts/) can carry action alternates too, so a hold on <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> can offer <KeyCap>Tab</KeyCap>, and a hold on any key can open voice typing or the editing pad. Every key can have alternates except the four that hold for something else already: <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>⌦</KeyCap> and <KeyCap>Space</KeyCap> hold to repeat, and a braille dot holds as part of its chord.

<PhoneFrame caption="Holding a letter opens its accent and symbol alternates.">
  ![Holding a letter opens its accent and symbol alternates.](@assets/screens/typing/long-press-popup.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

A few more long-press behaviors live under the same **Press and hold shortcuts** settings group:

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Press and hold shortcuts" />

- **All accents on press and hold** (off by default) adds each Latin letter's complete accent set to its long-press popup. Nothing is taken away to make room: the digit or symbol stays, with every accent beside it.
- **Press and hold ?123 for the number pad** (on by default) makes a long press on the symbols key open a full numeric keypad panel over the current field. A quick tap still switches to the symbols layer as usual.
- **Currency keys** lets you reorder or replace the glyphs the <KeyCap>$</KeyCap> key's popup offers (৳ € £ ¥ ₹ ₿ by default). Type them space-separated, with the one you use most first.

## Shift, caps lock, and auto-capitalization

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Timing" />

<KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> cycles through three states. From off, one tap arms a one-shot **Shift** that capitalizes only the next letter and then reverts on its own. A second tap inside the **caps-lock double-tap** window (350&nbsp;ms by default, adjustable 150–600&nbsp;ms) locks **Caps Lock** instead. From either state, a single tap that *isn't* a fast double-tap turns it back off. Getting out of caps lock takes one plain tap, not another double-tap.

<PhoneFrame caption="Shift off, one-shot Shift, and Caps Lock: the three states the key cycles through.">
  ![Shift off, one-shot Shift, and Caps Lock: the three states the key cycles through.](@assets/screens/typing/shift-states.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

Auto-capitalization arms Shift for you at the start of a sentence, but only when three things line up: **Automatic capital letters** is on (the default), the field is a plain text field rather than a structured keypad, and the active language's script has letter case at all. Bangla, for instance, doesn't, so auto-capitalize never fires while you type it. It re-checks after every sentence-ending mark (`.`, `!`, `?`, `।`) and whenever you move the cursor or switch fields. Caps Lock is off limits to it, so an all-caps line you locked in stays locked in.

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Automatic corrections" />

That toggle lives on the Typing screen rather than here. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/) for the rest of the automatic-corrections group it sits in.

## Hold a letter for a quick edit

<SettingsPath path="Key press / Press and hold shortcuts" />

Six letters can each be armed to run an editing command on long-press instead of opening their accent popup: <KeyCap>A</KeyCap> selects all, <KeyCap>C</KeyCap> copies, <KeyCap>X</KeyCap> cuts, <KeyCap>V</KeyCap> pastes, <KeyCap>Z</KeyCap> undoes, <KeyCap>Y</KeyCap> redoes. All six are **off by default**, and you turn on the ones you want one at a time. Once a letter's shortcut is on, it **replaces** that letter's accent popup on long-press. The two never combine, so a hold on `C` either shows `'çć` or copies, never both. Copy and cut select everything first if nothing is already selected, so a bare hold on `C` in an untouched field copies the whole field.

Those six keys are `a c v x z y` only because that is the QWERTY answer. Switch any of the shortcuts on and a **Shortcut keys** row joins the group, where you move all six onto keys your own layout actually draws. A Bengali or Russian grid has no `a` to hold, so without that row the switches would do nothing at all.

<PhoneFrame caption="With Press and hold C to copy turned on, holding C copies the selection instead of opening its accent popup.">
  ![With Press and hold C to copy turned on, holding C copies the selection instead of opening its accent popup.](@assets/screens/typing/hold-letter-clipboard.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

:::note
This is a different mechanism from <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>+letter on a physical keyboard, which has its own toggle for whether it sends the same clipboard action or a raw key event (some apps, like terminals, need the raw event). See [Hardware keyboards](/typing/hardware-keyboard/).
:::

## The Enter key

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Enter key" />

The key's icon and behavior follow whatever the focused field asks for: Search, Send, Go, Next, Previous or Done, each with its own icon, or a plain newline when the field doesn't specify. When an app supplies its own label instead of a standard action ("Reply", "Post"), the key draws that text directly rather than an icon.

<PhoneFrame caption="Enter drawing a field's own action icon.">
  ![Enter drawing a field's own action icon.](@assets/screens/typing/enter-action-icons.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

**Shift and Enter type a new line** (on by default) lets you press <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> then <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap>, or hold Shift on a physical keyboard, to force a line break in a field that would otherwise send or search. The key itself changes to the newline symbol while Shift is armed, so it never promises a Send it isn't about to do. Only a Shift you pressed yourself counts. The one-shot Shift that auto-capitalize arms at the start of a message is ignored, so the first line of a chat still sends normally, and Caps Lock is ignored too.

There's a second way in that doesn't need the setting at all: in any field where Enter has taken on the app's own action, press and hold the key. The popup that opens carries a newline entry, the same as Gboard's. Tap it and you get a line break, whatever the app declared. This one is always available and it never sends.

## Number row and numerals

<SettingsPath path="Layout & size / Number row" />

**Number row** (on by default) gives the digits 1–0 a dedicated row above the letters, so they don't need a long-press. While it's there, the digit that would otherwise be the top row's first long-press alternate is dropped, since the row makes it redundant. A few sub-settings sit under it. **Number row height** runs 32–100&nbsp;dp. **Symbols on shift** (off by default) swaps the row to `= \ < > [ ] { } | ~` while Shift is held, instead of digits. **Number row in symbols** (on by default) keeps the digit row up on the ?123 layer too. The symbols layer's own second page carries its arrow and comparison symbols either way.

<PhoneFrame caption="The number row above the letters.">
  ![The number row above the letters.](@assets/screens/typing/number-row.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

<SettingsPath path="Layout & size / Numerals" />

Separately, **numeral system** decides which *glyphs* the number row and keypad show, per language: Arabic can display ٠-٩ while English next to it stays on 0-9. Every language starts on **Auto**, which follows that language's own convention (Arabic → Arabic-Indic, Persian/Urdu → Persian, Bangla → Bengali, Devanagari-script languages → Devanagari, everything else → Latin). Override it per language on that language's own screen rather than here (see [Languages](/languages/overview/)).

This screen's **Type native digits in** setting answers a different question: whether those native glyphs are what actually gets *committed* when you type them. **Text fields**, the default, types the native glyphs everywhere except number, phone and date/time fields, which keep plain 0-9. **Everywhere** commits the native glyphs unconditionally. **Display only** always types plain ASCII digits, whatever the keys show.

## Smart key-hit detection

<SettingsPath path="Typing / Suggestions" />

**Smart detection of key hits** (on by default) quietly widens a key's touch target toward whichever letter the dictionary expects next as you type a word. A tap that lands just inside a neighboring key can still commit the letter you meant, but only near the shared edge. A deliberate press in the middle of a key is never second-guessed. It applies to the letters layer only, and it needs [Suggestions](/smart/suggestions/) turned on to have anything to predict from. See [Autocorrect](/smart/autocorrect/#reducing-typos-before-they-happen) for how it sits alongside autocorrect as an earlier line of defense against typos.

## Multi-touch and key repeat

Every finger on the keyboard is tracked on its own, so pressing a second key before you release the first works correctly. Both register, whether that's rollover typing across the letters or two fast taps on the very same key.

On the default layout, <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> and <KeyCap>Space</KeyCap> are the two keys that auto-repeat on a hold. Repeating has its own wait, **Time before a key repeats** (300&nbsp;ms by default, adjustable 150–800&nbsp;ms), separate from the long-press delay above. The two used to be one number, which meant lengthening the wait before accent popups also slowed backspace down. Past that wait each key fires again on its own cadence, **Delete repeat interval** and **Space repeat interval** (50&nbsp;ms and 100&nbsp;ms by default, both adjustable 20–200&nbsp;ms), because a runaway row of spaces costs more to undo than a fast delete does. A held backspace stops on its own once there's nothing left to delete. A forward-delete key (<KeyCap>⌦</KeyCap>) repeats the same way if you add one to a [custom layout](/languages/custom-layouts/). No built-in layout places one, and a physical keyboard's Delete-forward key goes straight to the system rather than through this engine. Every other key long-presses into its popup or shortcut instead of repeating.

<SettingsPath path="Accessibility / Touch" />

**Ignore repeated presses** (off by default) filters out the accidental double-taps a hand tremor causes. Auto-repeat deliberately bypasses that filter: the software repeat is already deterministic, so filtering it would only cap how fast delete or space can go.

Backspace also has a drag-to-delete-whole-words gesture, on by default, separate from this hold-and-repeat. [Gestures](/typing/gestures/) covers it.

## Details & edge cases

- **A hold-shortcut on A/C/X/V/Z/Y replaces that letter's accent popup rather than adding to it.** With "Press and hold C to copy" on, `c` no longer offers `'çć` on long-press at all.
- **Exiting Caps Lock only takes one tap.** The double-tap window matters only on the way *into* Caps Lock. Any single tap while Shift or Caps Lock is already active turns it off.
- **The Enter key never lies about what it's about to do.** With Shift + Enter's newline override active, the key visibly redraws to the newline symbol first. It doesn't swap behavior behind an unchanged icon.
- **A line break from the popup is a real one, even in apps that read Enter themselves.** Some chat apps watch for the Enter key press and send on it regardless of what the keyboard meant. The popup entry types the character straight into the field instead of pressing a key, so there's nothing for the app to catch. Shift + Enter works the same way.
- **The long-press delay is duplicated in two places.** It's a Key press → Timing slider (150–700&nbsp;ms), and again, framed for hand tremor, under Accessibility → Touch (150–800&nbsp;ms, a slightly wider range). Both sliders move the same underlying setting.
- **Number row height is independent of key height.** Shrinking the digit row doesn't touch the letter keys below it, so you can get quick number access without paying for a full extra row of keyboard height.
- **Ignore repeated presses only filters repeats of the *same* key.** Two different keys typed quickly are never blocked, so the setting can be raised without slowing normal typing down.
