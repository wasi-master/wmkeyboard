The **Clock** tool is a full clock app that lives in the keyboard. It keeps the time in the cities you care about, rings alarms and timers, runs a stopwatch, finds a meeting time that suits people in several time zones, counts days between dates, and keeps a history of all of it. And because it sits on a keyboard, every time it shows can go into the text you are writing, in the form you pick.

Open **Clock** from the toolbox, or type `clock`, `alarm`, `timer`, `stopwatch`, `countdown`, `planner` or `date` on its own and tap the chip that appears. The panel has seven tabs along the top: **Clocks**, **Alarms**, **Timers**, **Stopwatch**, **Planner**, **Dates** and **History**. A dot on a tab means something is running there, and a number counts the alarms that are on or the timers that are running. The panel opens on the tab you used last. Swipe left or right across the panel to move to the next or previous tab; the highlight slides from one tab to the next, and the row scrolls so the tab you landed on stays in view. The gear at the end of the row opens the tool's settings.

Every tab is laid out the same way: what matters most is large at the top, the rest is cards and tiles, and the one thing to do is a bar along the bottom with the main action as a wide button and the other ways beside it as round ones. Hold a round button to see its name. Cards and tiles pop in one after another when a tab opens; with **Reduce motion** on they appear at once.

The settings app has a **Clock** screen too, under <SettingsPath path="Tools / Clock / Open the clock" />. It works on the same alarms, timers, stopwatch, cities, countdowns and history, with a full-size time picker and a sound picker. A change made in one place shows in the other at once. **Add to home screen**, under **Open the clock**, puts an icon for that screen on your home screen, so it opens like a clock app.

## World clocks

The **Clocks** tab starts with your own time, large, with the date, the city your phone's time zone is named after, and a small clock face. The face is light by day and dark by night, and under the time are today's sunrise and sunset.

Under it, the cities you add ride a carousel of tiles that scrolls sideways. A tile is tinted by the daylight there: warm, in the keyboard's accent, where it is day, and dusky where it is night, with a sun or a moon in its corner and a stripe down its side in the same mood, so you can see at a glance who is awake. Each tile shows the city's name, its time, whether it is today, tomorrow or yesterday there, how many hours it is ahead of or behind you (`+5:30`, `−3`), and its sunrise and sunset. The last tile is **Add a city**.

**Add a city**, the wide button at the bottom or the last tile, opens a search, and the keyboard types into it. Type a city (`tokyo`, `sao pa`), a country, a zone abbreviation (`ist`, `pst`) or an offset (`+5:30`, `utc-3`). Accents don't matter. The list comes from the time zone data already on your phone, about 420 places, in your language where Android has a name for them. That data names each zone after one city, so about 60 big cities that share a zone with another are added by name: Beijing, Mumbai, New Delhi, Bengaluru, Osaka, San Francisco, Washington, Mecca and the like, in English, with older spellings such as `bangalore` or `bombay` finding them too. Each one keeps its own name on its card, and its own sunrise and sunset. Nothing is looked up online. Enter adds the first match.

Hold a city tile for its own row of chips under the carousel: type its time in the default format, move it left or right (unless **Sort cities by time** is on, which keeps the carousel in order of the time there, earliest first), rename it (to `Mum` or `Office`, say), or remove it. A removed city has an **Undo** for a few seconds.

### Typing a time

Tap your own clock or any city tile, and a row of chips opens under it (under the carousel, for a city). Each chip shows exactly what it will type, so you pick by looking:

| Format | Example |
|---|---|
| Time | `14:05` or `2:05 PM` |
| Date and time | `Wed 14 Oct, 14:05` |
| Full date | `Wednesday, 14 October 2026` |
| Time and place (the default) | `14:05 Tokyo (UTC+9)` |
| ISO 8601 | `2026-10-14T14:05:00+09:00` |
| Relative | `in 3 hours`, `2 days ago` |
| Unix time | `1791000000` |

Under those is a row for Discord timestamps. Discord turns `<t:1791000000:F>` into a time in each reader's own time zone and language, so one message works for everyone in the server. Every style Discord has gets a chip, showing roughly what readers will see:

| Discord style | Typed | Readers see |
|---|---|---|
| Short time | `<t:…:t>` | `10:23 PM` |
| Long time | `<t:…:T>` | `10:23:18 PM` |
| Short date | `<t:…:d>` | `10/18/2026` |
| Long date | `<t:…:D>` | `October 18, 2026` |
| Date and time | `<t:…:f>` | `October 18, 2026 at 10:23 PM` |
| Full date and time | `<t:…:F>` | `Sunday, October 18, 2026 at 10:23 PM` |
| Relative | `<t:…:R>` | `in 2 hours`, kept current as the message ages |
| Numeric | `<t:…:s>` | `10/18/2026, 10:23 PM` |
| Numeric with seconds | `<t:…:S>` | `10/18/2026, 10:23:18 PM` |

Every chip types the time now unless you pick another. **Another time** above the chips opens a time wheel set to the next whole hour, with arrows to step the day: tomorrow, next week, or a date you have to remember. The time is on the clock you tapped, so tapping the Kolkata card and picking 12:00 tomorrow gives 12:00 tomorrow in Kolkata, and a Discord chip then shows it to a reader in Karachi as 11:30. **Now** goes back to the current time.

Hold a chip to make it the default. Holding a Discord chip makes Discord the default in that style. After that, holding your own clock types the default straight away, without opening the chips, and the default is the first chip when you hold a city. **Insert all clocks**, the round button beside **Add a city**, types one line with every clock: `14:05 London = 9:05 New York = 18:35 Kolkata`. A city whose date is different from yours gets its weekday, so nobody turns up a day early.

## Alarms

The **Alarms** tab is a card per alarm: its time large, its label, the days it repeats as seven round pips (filled on the days it rings) and a switch. The alarm that rings next carries a line saying how long until it does ("Rings in 7 hr, 12 min"). An alarm with no days rings once, then turns itself off, like on any phone's clock. Tap a card to change it. Hold it for **Skip next**, which skips only the next ring of a repeating alarm, and **Delete**, which has an **Undo**.

Along the bottom, **New alarm** is the wide button, and above it a row of quick alarms for 10, 20 and 30 minutes and for one and one and a half hours from now. A quick alarm is set with one tap.

The editor puts the time on drums across the top that you spin with a finger, with a small tick at each step. Under them, a line says when the alarm will ring as you change it, then come the seven days, and one row with shortcuts for **Weekdays**, **Weekends** and **Every day**, a label, a vibration switch and the snooze length for this alarm. **Save** is the wide button at the bottom, with **Cancel** and **Delete** as round ones beside it.

### When an alarm rings

The phone lights up with the ring screen, also over the lock screen. It shows the time, the alarm's label and a pulsing bell. **Snooze** is the big button; smaller chips under it snooze for 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes instead. To dismiss, slide the thumb across the bar at the bottom. A slide rather than a tap means a sleepy hand can't turn an alarm off by accident. The volume keys snooze, unless you turn that off.

The sound starts quietly and gets louder over 20 seconds, then plays until you answer it. If nobody answers, it stops by itself after 10 minutes, shows as missed in the history and leaves a notification saying so. With **Snooze when nobody answers** set, it snoozes instead, a few times, before it counts as missed, or every time until you answer it when the slider is at **Until answered**, so an important alarm is still ringing when you come back to the phone. The shade carries the same **Snooze** and **Dismiss** buttons. A snoozed alarm stays in the shade with the time it rings again, and its **Dismiss** ends the snooze: a one-time alarm turns off, a repeating one waits for its next day. With **Notice before an alarm** on, the next alarm appears there ahead of time too, and **Skip** skips that one ring, or turns off a one-time alarm. Several alarms or timers ringing at once share one ring screen: the slide dismisses them all, and each one after the first also has its own **Dismiss**.

### What a reliable alarm needs

Android asks for three permissions before an app can ring an alarm the way a clock app does. The Alarms tab shows a line for any of them that is missing, and the tool's settings page lists them under **For alarms you can rely on**:

- **Alarms and reminders** (Android 12 and later). With it, alarms ring at the exact minute. Without it they still ring, but Android may hold one back by some minutes while the phone sleeps.
- **Full-screen notifications** (Android 14 and later, not in the Play Store build). With it, the ring screen takes over a locked phone. Without it, a ringing alarm shows as a notification instead.
- **Notifications** (Android 13 and later). For the Snooze and Dismiss buttons in the shade, and for running timers.

Each one opens with the keyboard's own explanation first, then Android's settings page for it. See [Permissions](/privacy/permissions/).

While something rings, the keyboard runs a short foreground service that plays the sound and keeps the phone awake. It is the only part of the app that runs that way, and it stops the moment you answer the ring or it gives up by itself.

### In the Play Store build

Google Play allows that kind of service, and the ring screen's full-screen permission, only for apps whose main job is an alarm clock, so the Play Store build has neither. There, an alarm or a timer rings through Android's own alarm notification instead. Snooze, Dismiss and the history work the same, and the ring still stops by itself after **Stop ringing after**. What changes:

- The ring shows as a notification at the top of the screen, not as the ring screen. Tap it to open the ring screen.

- The sound and the vibration are the ones set for the **Alarms ringing (backup)** notification in Android's settings. **Sound and vibration** in the tool's settings opens that page. There is no sound or vibration per alarm.
- There is no fade-in: the alarm starts at the volume Android gives alarm notifications.
- Timers ring with the same sound as alarms.

The GitHub and F-Droid builds have the service and every option above.

After you restart the phone, Android forgets every alarm an app has set. The keyboard sets them again when it next starts, which happens straight after a restart when it is your default keyboard. The app has no permission to start itself at boot.

## Timers

With no timer running, the **Timers** tab is a keypad. Digits fill from the right, the way a microwave's do: `1`, `3`, `0` is one minute thirty seconds. Under the reading are your presets; tap one to load it. Tap **Start**, the wide button at the bottom, and give the timer a label first with the round button beside it if you want to. The pill above the keypad switches between **Timer** and **Pomodoro**.

You can run as many timers as you like. Each one is a tile with a large ring that empties as the time runs out and the time left in the middle; under the ring are its name, when it ends (or **Paused**), and round buttons for **Pause**, **+1 min** and **Stop**. One timer takes the whole width; two or more sit two to a row. A paused timer can be reset to its full length. Your presets are a row of chips above the tiles, each starting a timer with one tap, and **New timer** at the bottom goes back to the keypad.

Presets start as 1, 3, 5, 10 and 25 minutes. With a time on the keypad, **Save as preset** adds it; hold a preset to delete it.

Running timers also show in the shade, counting down by themselves, with **Pause**, **+1 min** and **Stop**. When a timer runs out it rings like an alarm, at full volume, and **Snooze** on its ring screen becomes **Add 1 minute**.

### Pomodoro

Switch the keypad side to **Pomodoro** for focus sessions: 25 minutes of focus, a 5 minute break, again, and a 15 minute long break after every fourth focus session. **Start focus** begins the run. Each phase starts the next one by itself when it ends, so you only answer the ring. Dots under the timer show how far you are into the set of four. All four numbers are in the settings.

## Stopwatch

The **Stopwatch** tab shows the time to the hundredth of a second in large digits across the top, with a thin line under them that fills once a minute. Along the bottom, **Start** (or **Stop** while it runs) is the wide button, and beside it **Lap**, **Reset** and a button that types the time with all the laps into the field.

Laps are compact cards between the two, newest first, each with its own length, how much faster or slower it was than the lap before (`−0.31`, `+0.42`) and the total at that point. The fastest lap is green and the slowest is red.

The stopwatch keeps running with the keyboard hidden, and the shade has its **Lap** and **Pause** buttons. **Reset** puts the run in the history with its laps.

## Meeting planner

The **Planner** tab lays the same day out for your own city and every city on your list: one strip each, 24 hours across, in half hours. Working hours are the strong colour, waking hours the faint one, and night is nearly blank. The half hours where everyone is at work are brightest, and a soft band in the accent runs down every strip through them, so the good hours read as one column. Three tiles above the strips say how long that overlap lasts, the moment picked and how many of the places are at work then.

Drag along any strip, or tap it, and a cursor follows your finger with a tick at each half hour. Every city's time at that moment shows beside its name, with `+1` or `−1` where it is already tomorrow or still yesterday. Along the bottom, **Insert** is the wide button and types the moment in every city at once; **Discord** types it as one timestamp that each reader sees in their own time zone, in your default Discord style; and **Best time** jumps to the first half hour that suits the most people with nobody asleep. The arrows at the top move to other days.

Working hours are 9:00 to 17:00 unless you change them in the settings. They apply to every city.

## Dates

The **Dates** tab does date maths with steppers, and shows each answer as tiles. **Between**, **Add** and **Countdowns** sit on one pill at the top. Each date has buttons for a month back, a day back, a day on and a month on; tap the date itself to go back to today. For a date far away, such as a birthday years back, press and hold the date and type it in the box that opens, such as `17/5/2004` or `2004-05-17`. The day comes first unless your phone is set to a country that writes the month first, like the US; a date that only makes sense the other way round is read that way, and a two-digit year up to 50 is this century.

- **Between** shows three tiles: the days between the two dates (with the same span as years, months and days under it), the weeks, and the working days (Monday to Friday). The arrow between the steppers swaps the two dates.
- **Add** moves a date by days, weeks, months, years or working days, and shows the result as a card with the date, beside tiles with its week number and its day of the year. A month added to 31 January gives the last day of February.
- **Countdowns** is a grid of cards for the days you are counting towards (a trip, an exam), each with how many days are left, or how many have passed. Tap one to type it, such as `23 days until Exam`. Hold one for **Delete**. **New countdown**, the wide button at the bottom, asks for the date and a name; **Save** and **Cancel** take its place while you set it.

**Insert**, the wide button at the bottom, types the result of the Between or Add view.

## History

The **History** tab is a timeline of what rang and what was timed, newest first: a rail down the left names each day (**Today**, **Yesterday**, then dates) and the day's entries are compact cards beside it, each with its kind, its name, its length, its outcome and its time. It covers alarms (dismissed, snoozed or missed), finished and stopped timers, Pomodoro sessions, and stopwatch runs. A stopwatch run opens to show its laps. Above the timeline, two tiles hold today's focus time from Pomodoro and the total time you timed. Filter by kind with the chips; the bin clears what the filter shows, with **Undo**. The button on each card types it.

A timer stopped within its first five seconds, or a stopwatch reset before five seconds, is not kept. History is kept for 90 days by default, and never more than 500 entries.

The tool's settings live in the phone's device storage rather than with the keyboard's other settings, because the alarm has to read them before you unlock the phone after a restart. A [backup](/reference/settings/backup/) carries them in its **Clock** section, together with the alarms, the presets and the countdowns.

The clock and the calendar are the **Calendar and clock** bundle on [Extensions](/reference/settings/extensions/). Off there, both tools and this screen leave the keyboard and the app until the bundle is switched back on; your clocks and countdowns stay.

## Details & edge cases

- **Alarms ring before the first unlock.** Alarms, timers and the clock's settings are kept in the part of the phone's storage that is readable before you unlock it after a restart, so a 6:30 alarm rings even if the phone restarted overnight. The keyboard panel itself waits for the unlock, like the other tools that keep files.
- **Daylight saving time.** An alarm set for a time that does not exist on the night the clocks go forward rings at the first minute that does, usually an hour later. On the night they go back, it rings the first time that hour comes round.
- **Changing the clock or the time zone** moves every alarm with it: they ring at their time on the clock that is now showing.
- **Timers follow the phone's clock.** A timer keeps running when the keyboard is closed and survives the keyboard being stopped, because its end time is stored. If you change the phone's clock by hand while one runs, it ends early or late by that much.
- **A timer that ran out while the keyboard was not running** rings as soon as the keyboard starts again, rather than not at all.
- **Two phones don't ring for one alarm.** [Sync](/reference/settings/backup/) leaves the Clock section out unless you add it. The world clocks are part of the Clock section, not of the keyboard's settings.
- **Nothing goes online.** The city search, the sunrise and sunset times and every calculation run on the phone. The sunrise and sunset for a city come from the city's coordinates in the time zone data, so they are for the city the zone is named after, except for the added cities like Beijing, which carry their own.
- **The planner's working hours are the same for every city.** A city with a different working week or hours still uses yours.
