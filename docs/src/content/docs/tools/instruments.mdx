"Instruments" is this site's name for five small utility tools that Settings groups together as "Utilities," alongside [Calendar](/tools/calendar/) and [Plugins](/plugins/overview/): **Flashlight**, **Compass**, **Bubble level**, **Moon phase** and **Weather**. None of them makes you leave the keyboard to check something. Four open as a panel that replaces the key rows. Flashlight is the odd one out, a single tap with no panel at all. The same group also holds [Screenshot](#screenshot), another single tap.

<PhoneFrame caption="The Utilities group under Settings → Tools: Flashlight, Compass, Bubble level, Calendar, Weather, Moon phase and Plugins.">
  <Shot id="tools/instruments-settings-utilities-group" alt="The Utilities group under Settings → Tools: Flashlight, Compass, Bubble level, Calendar, Weather and Moon phase." />
</PhoneFrame>

Open any of the five from the toolbar if you've pinned it, or from the toolbox. None ships pinned by default, and none is in the onboarding wizard's Recommended set, so the first time you want one you'll probably find it in the toolbox. Weather is the one the "show me everything" answer does turn on, next to Calendar. The toolbox doesn't label a "Utilities" section the way Settings does, and it doesn't keep the five together either. Flashlight, Compass, Bubble level and Moon phase sit as a block near the end of the default ranking, after the scanners and the camera and just before the one-tap cursor moves. Weather is much earlier, right after Calendar in the everyday run.

None of the five needs an Android runtime permission. The torch doesn't need the `CAMERA` permission (that's only for the separate [camera tool](/tools/camera/)). Weather's location is typed or searched, or read once from the phone when you tap **Use my location**, which asks for location access the first time; see [Location](/tools/location/#weather-and-compass). No build is short-changed either: every build behaves identically across all five.

## Flashlight: a one-tap torch

Tapping **Flashlight** turns the device's torch on or off immediately. There's no panel to open or close. If the device has no flash, a toast says "Your device has no flashlight" and nothing else happens.

The toolbox tile mirrors the torch's real state. Flip the torch on from the system quick-settings tile instead of the keyboard and the Flashlight tile lights up to match. Turning it off from either place turns it off in both.

  <Shot id="tools/flashlight-toggled-on" alt="Flashlight is a direct toggle: no panel opens, and the toolbox tile lights up while the torch is on." />
</PhoneFrame>

### Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Flashlight" />

- **Turn off with the keyboard**: on by default. On, closing the keyboard or switching apps switches off a torch you turned on from the keyboard, so it's never left burning in your pocket. A torch you turned on from the quick-settings tile or another app is left alone. Off, the torch stays on until you toggle it again from the tool or the system quick-settings tile.

## Compass, with an optional qibla bearing

**Compass** draws a live rose from the device's rotation-vector sensor, with a fixed needle at the top marking the way you're actually facing. The heading glides smoothly rather than jumping with every sensor sample. On a device with no orientation sensor at all, the panel says so instead of drawing an empty rose.

Turn on **Show the qibla** and a 🕋 marker rides the rose, pointing toward the Kaaba. The compass has no location field of its own. It reuses whatever location you've already saved in the [Weather](#weather) tool's settings, and without one saved the panel prints "Qibla needs a saved location. Save one in the settings of the weather tool." instead of a bearing, with a **Use my location** button under it that reads your position once and saves it as that location. Everything is calculated on-device from that saved latitude and longitude. The compass itself never touches the network.

The [Location tool](/tools/location/#places-somebody-sent-you) can also point the compass at any place, a friend's pin, say. A 📍 marker rides the rose next to the 🕋 one, and a line under the rose gives its distance and direction, with **Stop pointing** beside it.

Once a location is saved, the rose turns to true north rather than magnetic north. The phone's sensor finds magnetic north, which is a few degrees off true north in most places and up to twenty in some; Android's own model of the Earth's field gives the difference for the saved place, so the heading and the qibla marker are both measured from true north.

### Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Compass" />

- **Degree readout**: on by default. It shows the numeric heading and a 16-point cardinal (N, NNE, NE, … NNW) under the rose. Before the sensor has reported it reads "Calibrating…" instead.
- **Show the qibla**: off by default. Turn it on before saving a location and the settings screen shows a caution of its own: "No location is saved yet. Set one under Tools → Weather."

## Bubble level

**Bubble level** turns the accelerometer, or the gravity sensor on a device that has one, into three gauges at once. A circular bullseye bubble combines pitch and roll, and a vertical and a horizontal tube vial each track one axis alone. The bullseye turns accent-colored once both pitch and roll are within about a degree of flat. Each tube vial turns accent-colored independently on its own single-axis threshold, so a vial can read level a moment before or after the bullseye agrees.

<PhoneFrame caption="Bubble level: a bullseye bubble plus a vertical and horizontal tube vial, with an angle readout below.">
  <Shot id="tools/level-panel" alt="Bubble level: a bullseye bubble plus a vertical and horizontal tube vial, with an angle readout below." />
</PhoneFrame>

### Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Bubble level" />

- **Angle readout**: on by default. It shows pitch and roll in degrees below the gauges, colored to match the bullseye's flat/not-flat state.

## Moon phase

**Moon phase** is arithmetic rather than a lookup. The panel computes the current phase from a fixed synodic-month constant and a reference new moon, with nothing fetched over the network. It's accurate to within a few hours, which is plenty for "is it worth looking up tonight" and not built for precise astronomical prediction.

The panel shows a painted lit/dark disc, one of eight phase names (New moon, Waxing crescent, First quarter, Waxing gibbous, Full moon, Waning gibbous, Last quarter, Waning crescent), the percentage illuminated, and how many days into the roughly 30-day cycle you are. Under that is a short timetable of the four main phases (new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter), each with its day and time: the one that has just passed, in a dimmer color, then the next three.

<PhoneFrame caption="Moon phase: the current lit fraction, phase name, and next full and new moon dates, all computed on-device.">

</PhoneFrame>

### Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Moon phase" />

- **Arabic names too** (off): shows each phase's Arabic name as well, under the name in your language and beside each phase in the timetable: محاق, هلال, تربيع أول, أحدب متزايد, بدر, أحدب متناقص, تربيع ثانٍ and هلال متناقص.
- **Southern hemisphere**: read off your phone's region, so it starts on in Australia, Brazil or South Africa and off in Bangladesh or the UK. It mirrors the lit disc the way the moon actually looks south of the equator. Only the drawing changes, never the phase name, dates or illumination percentage.

## Weather

**Weather** shows current conditions and a same-day high/low for one saved location, sourced from [Open-Meteo](https://open-meteo.com). No account and no API key are needed. You set the location yourself: search a city or place name (which hits Open-Meteo's geocoding endpoint), type latitude and longitude directly, or tap **Use my location** to read the phone's position once. Only that last one asks for location access, and only the first time you use it. The saved place doesn't follow you afterwards; it stays put until you change it.

A fetched result is reused for as long as **How often to check** says, 15 minutes out of the box, so reopening the panel is usually instant. A refresh button in the panel forces a new request. Until you save a location the panel shows a prompt with an **Open settings** shortcut and a **Use my location** button instead of any weather data. If a fetch fails it shows a retry button rather than stale numbers.

<PhoneFrame side caption="Weather: current conditions plus a stat grid of feels-like, humidity, wind, and whatever else Open-Meteo returns for that location.">
  <Shot id="tools/weather-panel-ready" alt="Weather: current conditions plus a stat grid: feels-like, humidity, wind, and whatever else Open-Meteo returns for that location." />
</PhoneFrame>

Once a location is set, the panel shows an emoji and short description for the current conditions, the place name with today's high and low, and a wrapping grid of stats. Feels-like temperature, humidity, and wind speed with an 8-point cardinal direction always appear. Rain chance, cloud cover, pressure, UV index, sunrise and sunset each appear only when Open-Meteo's response actually includes them.

### Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Weather" />

<PhoneFrame caption="Weather's settings: a location search or coordinate entry, and the Fahrenheit toggle.">
  <Shot id="tools/weather-options-location" alt="Weather's settings: a location search or coordinate entry, and the Fahrenheit toggle." />
</PhoneFrame>

- **Location**: unset by default. It opens a dialog with **Use my location** at the top, a search field backed by Open-Meteo's geocoding API, and manual latitude and longitude fields if you'd rather skip both. The same dialog adds places in the [Location](/tools/location/#saved-places) tool.
- **Fahrenheit**: off by default, so temperatures show in Celsius until you turn this on. It moves the current, feels-like, and day's high and low temperatures. **Wind speed follows this switch too**: km/h with Celsius, mph with Fahrenheit. Pressure stays in hPa, which has no second unit here.
- **Fetch the forecast as you type**: on by default, off in the F-Droid edition. On, a weather chip fetches the forecast it needs as soon as you type a weather question. Off, the chip reads **Get the forecast** and fetches nothing until you tap it. The panel fetches when you open it either way.
- **How often to check**: how long a reading is reused before the tool fetches again. 1 to 180 minutes, default 15. The panel's refresh button still forces a fetch whatever this says.

Those four rows are the whole screen. The settings screen carries its own privacy note underneath: "The weather and the place search both use Open-Meteo, only when you use them. The keyboard makes no other network requests." Weather is the only one of the five instruments that reaches the network at all. The one exception to that note is **Use my location** with the Location tool's address lookup on, which asks the address server for the city's name once. See [network policy](/privacy/network/) for what every network-touching feature sends and when.

The flashlight, the compass, the bubble level and the moon phase are the **Instruments** bundle on [Extensions](/reference/settings/extensions/), and weather is part of **Weather and location**. A bundle that is off takes its tools off the toolbar, the toolbox and the Tools list until it is switched back on.

## Screenshot

**Screenshot** takes a screenshot of the app you're in, the same one the power and volume keys take. The keyboard goes down first, so it isn't in the picture, and stays down afterwards. Android saves the screenshot where every screenshot goes and shows its usual preview, with its own editing and sharing. Bind it to the [floating button](/reference/settings/floating-button/#gestures) and the button steps out of the picture too, coming back a moment later.

Android lets another app take a screenshot only through an accessibility service, so the tool needs one of its own, **WM Keyboard screenshots**, turned on in Android's accessibility settings. It does one thing, presses Android's screenshot button when you press the tool, and reads nothing on screen. The first press while it's off explains this and then opens Android's accessibility settings; the tool's page under <SettingsPath path="Tools / Screenshot" /> has a **Screenshot service** row that says whether it's on and takes you there too. See [Permissions](/privacy/permissions/).

It needs Android 9 or later, and it isn't in the Play Store build, which ships without accessibility services that aren't its main purpose. It doesn't work before your first unlock after a restart.

## Details & edge cases

- **None have a default hardware-keyboard letter.** Tools such as web search (<KeyCap>W</KeyCap>) and dictionary (<KeyCap>D</KeyCap>) ship with a leader-key letter, and none of these five do. Reach them through the toolbox shortcut (double-tap <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>, then <KeyCap>T</KeyCap>) or [rebind a letter to one yourself](/reference/shortcuts/).
- **Four keep working before your first unlock. Weather doesn't.** Flashlight, Compass, Bubble level and Moon phase are all direct-boot safe. They're pure sensor readings or arithmetic, so they work right after a restart, before you've entered your PIN or pattern for the first time. Weather is the exception: it needs network access and reads a location saved in credential-encrypted storage, and neither is available until you unlock.
- **Every tool here has more than a bare switch.** Each of the five gets a "Tune" icon in <SettingsPath path="Tools" />, meaning it has real options beyond the shared Enabled toggle. None of them is one of the toggle-only tools on that screen.
- **Colored tool icons** under <SettingsPath path="Tools" /> is on out of the box, so each of these five already wears its own accent color in the toolbox and in the Tools list itself. The pinned toolbar row keeps the theme's plain icon color regardless, same as every other tool.

For the toolbar and toolbox mechanics these five share with every other tool (pinning, the toolbox drag, and what "direct-boot safe" means more broadly), see [the toolbar](/tools/overview/).
