The Calendar tool opens a full month grid inside the keyboard, or the same month as an agenda down a day rail. Browse any month, and up to two non-Gregorian calendars ride alongside the Gregorian dates over the grid and inside each day cell. Tap a day to see its date across every calendar you've picked, plus whatever's on your device calendar for that day as a row of cards. You can also add an event, change or delete one (repeating ones included), invite guests, and type an event into the app you're writing in, all without leaving the keyboard.

## Browsing the month

Open **Calendar** from the toolbar, or from the toolbox if you haven't pinned it. The header carries the month and year as its title, a **Today** chip, a **Month | Agenda** switch, a round **+** for a new event and the tool's settings. Swipe sideways across the grid to step a month: the grid slides out and the next one slides in. Tap the title to drop a month picker instead, with the year between two arrows and the twelve months as chips, so a date far away is two taps rather than a dozen swipes. **Today** jumps straight back and reselects today. The weekday row along the top tints your weekend in the accent color, and which days those are is a setting that starts off from your phone's region (see [Options](#options)).

Tapping a day cell doesn't leave the panel. It updates the summary and the cards below the grid. The selected day sits on an accent disc that springs across the grid to whichever day you tap next, today's cell has a thin accent ring, and a small dot sits under every day that has something on. On the selected day the dots take the colors of its events' calendars. Press and hold a day to start a new event on it.

If either of your two chosen calendars has day numbers that don't line up one-to-one with the Gregorian grid, its day number rides inside the cell as a small second number next to the Gregorian one. Bengali dates render in Bengali numerals (০–৯). The first day of a Chinese lunar month shows the month name instead of a bare "1", since that's the only cue in the grid that a lunar month just turned over.

Below the grid, the selected day shows its full Gregorian date, weekday and a relative label (Today, Tomorrow, Yesterday, "in N days," or "N days ago") on one line, and the same date in whichever alternate calendars you've picked under it.

## Today's events

The bottom of the panel reads events straight from your device calendar for whichever day is selected. Until you grant calendar access, it shows a one-line prompt with an **Allow** button instead of any event data. Once granted, it queries in the background every time you change the selected day: a blank row while loading, "No events" with a **New event** button if the day is clear, or a row of event cards you swipe through sideways otherwise. The cards pop in one after another each time you pick a day.

Each card is tinted with its calendar's color and carries a stripe of it down the left edge (the accent color where the calendar doesn't provide one). The time runs along the top as a start and end, "9:00 – 10:00 AM", with an **All day** badge in its place for all-day events and a small repeat mark on the right for recurring ones; then the title, then the place if the event has one. Recurring events are expanded per occurrence, so a weekly meeting shows up on every day it actually lands on rather than only its first occurrence. Events in calendars you've hidden in your calendar app stay hidden here too.

Tap a card to open the event. The event's own face shows it as a larger card in the same color with when and the title, then how it repeats, the place, notes, guests (with who said yes, no or maybe) and reminders as lines under it; the calendar it's in is named in the header. Along the bottom runs a bar: **Edit** as the main button when you can change the event, with **Insert as text**, **Open in calendar app** and **Delete** as round buttons beside it. For an event you can't change, **Insert as text** takes the main spot.

## Agenda

The **Agenda** side of the header's switch shows the same month as a list. A rail down the left names each day that has something on (**Today**, **Tomorrow**, **Yesterday**, or the date), with a dot on the rail per day and the accent dot on today, and the day's events sit beside it as the same tinted cards. The day you've selected is the one whose cards are loaded, with a ring around its dot; a day you haven't opened yet shows a quiet card with its date that loads it when tapped, and once a day has been opened its cards stay while you move around the month. Swiping sideways moves a month here too. A month with nothing on offers **New event** in the middle, and until calendar access is granted the agenda shows the **Allow** prompt in its place.

## Adding an event

Tap the round **+** in the header, or press and hold a day in the grid. The composer opens on the selected day with the title already taking your typing, and the panel folds down so the keys show underneath; while you type, the folded panel shows the event's preview card so you keep your bearings. **Done** (or Enter) moves you from the title to the place.

The composer is a preview card over a tray of chips. The card is the event as it will look in the month, tinted with its calendar's color: when it is, its title (or **Title** as a hint until you type one), its place, and a line in the accent color when it overlaps something already in your calendar ("Overlaps Standup"). It updates as you change anything. A tap on the card sends the keys back to the title.

Under it, each piece of the event is a chip that reads what it holds, or its hint while empty: the when, **Add a place**, **Add guest** (or how many guests), how it repeats, the reminders, which calendar (with its color dot), **Add notes**, and **Busy** or **Free**. Tapping a text chip sends the keys to it. Tapping any other unfolds its editor under the tray, one at a time, and the open chip is drawn selected:

- **When** holds **All day**, which turns the times off so the start and end name whole days; **Starts** and **Ends**, each with a date chip that opens a small month and a time chip that opens the same spinning drums the Clock tool uses; and the length chips (15 min up to 3 h), which set the end for you. Moving the start moves the end with it, so the length stays the same. If the end lands before the start, the **Ends** label and the card's time turn the accent color and the main button refuses until it's fixed. When the event's time zone isn't the phone's, a chip names the zone the times are in.
- **Busy** or **Free** flips on a tap.
- **Repeat**, **Reminders**, the calendar and guests each have their own editor; see below.

A new event starts with the length and reminder you set in [Options](#options), in your default calendar. The bar along the bottom holds **Add to calendar** as the main button (**Save changes** when you're editing), with **Insert as text** and **Open in calendar app** as round buttons beside it. The event goes straight into your calendar, the panel goes back to the month with that day selected, and an **Undo** bar takes it back out again if you change your mind. **Cancel** in the header throws the draft away.

If you close the panel halfway through, the draft is kept. Open the tool again and a **Resume** line at the top brings it back.

### Reminders

The reminders chip lists what's set, or **No reminder** while there are none. Tapping it unfolds the choices: at the start, 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, 1 or 2 hours, 1, 2 or 7 days before, with the ones already set drawn selected. A tap adds or removes one. Some calendar accounts limit how many reminders an event can carry, and the remaining choices grey out once you reach that limit.

### Repeating events

The repeat chip unfolds a set of chips worded for the event's own day: daily, weekly on that weekday, every weekday (Monday to Friday), monthly on that date, monthly on its weekday ("the third Monday"), monthly on the last such weekday when it falls in the last week, and yearly. **More options** opens the rest: repeat every 2 weeks or every 3 months, pick several weekdays, and end it never, on a date, or after a number of times.

Move a weekly event to another day and its repeat day moves too, so a Monday meeting dragged to Tuesday keeps landing on Tuesdays. A repeat rule written by another app that this editor can't show (several dates a month, say) appears as **Custom repeat** and is saved back exactly as it was unless you pick a different rule.

### Guests

The guests chip unfolds the guest list and **Add guest**, which opens a box for a name or an email address. With contacts allowed, typing a name lists the matching people with their addresses, and a tap adds one. Without contacts, type the whole address and press Enter. Each guest is a chip with a cross to take them off again.

Guests are written to the event the same way your calendar app writes them, which means your calendar account (Google Calendar, for one) may email them an invitation when it next syncs. The composer says so under the guest list. A calendar that only lives on your phone sends nothing.

### Insert as text

The **Insert as text** button types the event into the app you're writing in, as one line: the title, the date and time in your own format, and the place, like "Standup, Mon 19 Oct, 9:00 – 10:00 AM · Room 4". It's on the bar of the event's face and on the composer's, so you can share a plan before or after saving it.

## Changing and deleting events

Open an event and tap **Edit**. The composer opens with everything filled in, including its guests, reminders and repeat rule, and its main button reads **Save changes**, which writes them back to the same event. **Delete** on the event's bar removes it, with **Undo** afterwards.

Events you can't change show a note instead of Edit and Delete: one you were invited to by someone else (unless they let guests edit), or one in a read-only calendar such as holidays or a shared calendar you can only view.

### Repeating events

Changing or deleting one occurrence of a repeating event first asks which ones you mean:

- **This event** changes or removes just that day. The rest of the series is left alone.
- **This and following** ends the series the day before and, for a change, starts a new series from that day with your edits. A series that was set to run a number of times keeps the right total between the two halves.
- **All events** changes the whole series. A new time or a new day moves the series' first occurrence by the same amount, so a series that began in March still begins in March.

If you changed the repeat rule itself, **This event** isn't offered, since a single day can't repeat. **Undo** can put back a single changed or removed day, a series that was cut short, and a deleted event.

## Calendar permission

The keyboard asks for calendar access in two steps, and never at install or first launch. Reading comes first: tap **Allow** in the panel and you can see your events. Writing comes the first time you save or delete an event, and is asked for together with reading. Your event waits while the dialog is up, and is saved as soon as you come back with the grant. Refuse it and the composer says so and offers **Open in calendar app** instead, which hands the event to your calendar app's own editor to save there.

An on-screen keyboard service can't request runtime permissions directly. So each of these opens an invisible trampoline activity that shows a short explanation of what the access is for, then the system permission dialog, then closes itself the instant you answer. The [camera tool](/tools/camera/) uses the same pattern for its own permission. If you revoke calendar access in system Settings and come back to the keyboard, the panel notices as soon as it regains focus and drops back to the Allow prompt.

Guest suggestions ask for contacts access the same way, from the **Suggest from contacts** link under the guest box. The tool reads names and addresses only while that box is open and keeps nothing afterwards.

The tool writes only what you save, change or delete yourself. Nothing is changed in the background.

:::note[Not available before you unlock]
Calendar is left out of the keyboard's direct-boot-safe tool set. It has to query a content provider that isn't available before your first unlock after a restart. Open the toolbox on a still-locked device and Calendar isn't listed there at all, so there's no broken panel to walk into. The arithmetic and sensor tools (the calculator, the unit converter, the compass, the level, the moon phase) are still there, but anything that reads the calendar provider is not.
:::

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Calendar" />

Like every tool, Calendar opens with a **Turn on** switch and, while [colorful tool icons](/reference/settings/tools/) are on globally, an **Icon colour** override (a soft red by default). Under those sits the **Keyword shortcut** group: **Trigger words**, pre-filled with "calendar, schedule", a **Match capitals** toggle off by default, and a **Reset to defaults** row once you've edited the words. Then the tool's own settings:

  <Shot id="tools/calendar-options" alt="Calendar options: First and second calendar pickers, and the Hijri day adjustment slider." />
</PhoneFrame>

- **First calendar**: the default depends on your phone's region. That's the Bengali calendar in Bangladesh, era years in Japan, Hindu (Saka) in India, Hijri across much of the Arab world, and **None** where no second calendar is in common use. Choose from Bengali, Hijri, Chinese, Hebrew, Hindu (Saka), Persian (Solar Hijri), Buddhist (Thai solar), Japanese (era years), or None. Its day number is the one that also shows up inside each day cell. Buddhist and Japanese days line up one-to-one with Gregorian, so if you pick either the cell falls back to Second calendar's day number instead, where that one has its own.
- **Second calendar**: the same nine choices, also region-defaulted. Bangladesh gets Hijri alongside the Bengali calendar, Japan gets the Chinese calendar alongside era years, and most places get **None**. It shows in the month header and under the selected day, alongside First calendar.
- **Weekend**: which weekday initials the month grid tints. Seven presets: Saturday & Sunday, Friday & Saturday, Thursday & Friday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday, or no weekend at all. The default comes from your phone's region: Friday and Saturday across much of the Middle East, Friday in Bangladesh, Thursday and Friday in Iran and Afghanistan, Saturday in Nepal, Sunday alone in India, Mexico and the Philippines, and Saturday and Sunday everywhere else.
- **Hijri day adjustment**: a slider from −2 to +2 days, default 0. It only appears when First or Second calendar is set to Hijri. The tool computes Hijri dates with the tabular (arithmetic) calendar rather than real moon sighting, so real Islamic months can start a day or two off from the tables. Use this to match your local authority's sighting-based calendar.

Under **New events**:

- **Save events**: **In the keyboard** (the default) writes new events straight into your calendar. **In the calendar app** makes Save open your calendar app's editor with the event filled in, for you to save there; it also makes the "Add to calendar" [selection action](/smart/selection-actions/) go to the calendar app. Changing and deleting events always happens in the keyboard.
- **Default calendar**: which calendar a new event starts in. **Primary calendar** picks the first one you can write to, primary calendars first. Until calendar access is allowed, the row offers to ask for it.
- **Event length**: 15 minutes up to 4 hours, default 1 hour.
- **Reminder**: none, at the start, or 5 minutes up to 1 day before, default 10 minutes.

Accuracy varies by calendar: Chinese dates are computed astronomically (real new-moon and solar-longitude calculations), so they're exact for the modern era. Hebrew, Persian, Hindu (Saka), Buddhist, and Japanese are all exact arithmetic conversions. Hijri is the only one that's an approximation, which is why it's the only one with an adjustment slider.

The same First calendar / Second calendar / Weekend pickers also appear on the first-run setup page during onboarding, if you've kept Calendar enabled: anything you set there is the same setting, just asked up front. All three already open on the region's guess, so that page is where you see it and correct it.

The calendar and the clock are the **Calendar and clock** bundle on [Extensions](/reference/settings/extensions/). Off there, both tools and the clock's settings screen leave the keyboard and the app until the bundle is switched back on.

## Details & edge cases

- Insert as text types an event in Gregorian dates only. The alternate calendars are shown in the panel but not inserted.
- Selecting a date or time in your text and using **Add to calendar** from the [selection actions](/smart/selection-actions/) opens this composer with the selection as the title and the date and time already set. A time zone named in the selection ("5pm EST") is kept, and the composer shows which zone the times are in.
- Opened from a date [smart chip](/smart/chips/) ("next friday"), the panel lands on that day, and the header's **+** starts a new event there.
- The month grid, the agenda and the composer play their motion (the disc's spring, the cards popping in, the grid's slide) only while the keyboard's animations are on; with reduced motion everything lands in place at once.
- Calendar isn't pinned to the toolbar out of the box, and it's only in the "show me everything" starter set that setup lands, not the minimal or the recommended one. Pick either of those two simpler answers and Calendar starts switched off, so you'll want to turn it on yourself under Settings → Tools.
- Reopening Calendar from the toolbar while the panel is already open closes it, same as most other panel tools.
- **The region defaults are a guess, and only ever a starting one.** They come from the same permission-free signals as [suggested languages](/languages/overview/#suggested-languages): the SIM's country, then the network's, then your locale's region. Those are consulted only until you pick something. Once you have, your pick is what sticks, wherever the phone travels.
- Nothing about the Calendar tool touches the network. Your calendar account syncs the events you add or change the way it always does. See [network policy](/privacy/network/) for what the keyboard itself sends.
