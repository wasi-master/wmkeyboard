Offline Whisper is a second dictation engine that runs entirely on your phone. Instead of streaming audio to a speech-recognition service, it records what you say and transcribes it locally with an OpenAI Whisper model, so nothing ever leaves the device.

For English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch and Polish there is also [offline Whistle](/tools/whistle/): one 17 MB model that answers faster, where Whisper has a catalog that covers far more languages. For English alone, [offline Asr4All](/tools/asr4all/) writes the words while you are still speaking.

## Why offline dictation

The system recognizer, covered in [Voice typing](/tools/voice/), is fast and shows words as you speak. It also leans on your OS and its installed languages, and without the on-device language pack it sends audio to a recognizer service. Offline Whisper trades all of that away for a hard guarantee: it works across many languages and never sends audio anywhere.

You feel that trade every time you dictate, because Whisper doesn't stream. It records into a buffer and transcribes the whole clip once you stop talking, or once you hit the 30-second cap (see [Details & edge cases](#details--edge-cases)). The panel shows "Listening. Press when you are done." rather than live partial words. That press doesn't need aim: the whole panel left of the action rail takes it, so does anywhere on the compact bar, and so does the Voice tool on the toolbar (see [Starting dictation](/tools/voice/#starting-dictation)).

## Two kinds of model

Offline Whisper runs two kinds of model, and the catalog lists both.

- **whisper.cpp models** run on [whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp), which is built into the app. They are the ones pointed at first, and they can do everything below that the other kind can't:
  - **Any language, by name.** Each of Whisper's 99 languages, Bangla included, can be told to the model instead of left for it to guess. A dictation in [several languages](/tools/voice/#language-and-engine) is detected among those languages only, so a short phrase is never read as some third one.
  - **Your own words.** The same **Words to listen for** list the system recognizer gets goes to the model as a prompt, so names and terms come out spelled your way.
  - **Words while it works.** The line under the microphone shows the transcript as it is decoded, instead of only saying "Transcribing".
  - **Longer clips.** A clip can run to two minutes instead of 30 seconds.
  - **A closer search** on the smaller sizes (beam search), which gets a few more words right, and a transcription you cancel stops at once instead of finishing for nobody.
  - Each one is a single `.bin` file, checked against its published SHA-256 once it downloads.
- **LiteRT models** are the community `.tflite` conversions the app started with. Each has the whole decoding loop compiled in, so none of the above can be asked of it, and a few can only be told one of a fixed set of languages. They still work, and a model you already downloaded keeps working. Where whisper.cpp is available their names end in "(LiteRT)".

whisper.cpp is in every build, on 64-bit and 32-bit ARM phones and on x86_64. A phone whose cores all have the ARMv8.2 dot-product instructions (most from 2018 on) gets a build that uses them, which is several times faster at the arithmetic these models are made of. On a phone with a Qualcomm Snapdragon chip (an Adreno GPU), the whisper.cpp models can also run on the GPU; see [Running on the GPU](#running-on-the-gpu).

Bigger models trade download size and speed for accuracy:

- **Tiny**: fastest multilingual model. Fine for clear speech, struggles with noise.
- **Base**: balanced accuracy and speed.
- **Small** *(recommended)*: the best accuracy that still runs comfortably on a phone, noticeably better on accents and background noise than Base.
- **Medium**: near-desktop accuracy. Expect several seconds per phrase and close to a gigabyte of RAM while it runs.
- **Turbo**: large-v3-turbo, so it's about as accurate as Large at half the download, and much better than the small models at working out which language you're speaking. An 820 MB download. It was trained for transcription only, so translation isn't offered for it.
- **Large**: the most accurate graphs there are, and far too big for most phones. A 1.5 GB download that only high-memory devices can load. Two are listed, and if you're going to spend the 1.5 GB, **Large v3** is the one to take: it's the newer model and it recognises languages much more reliably than the original Large.

## The model catalog

On the Play Store build the interpreter that runs the LiteRT models is not in the app you install: it is a small extra part (about 2 MB) that Google Play delivers the first time you need it. The Whisper settings screen offers it at the top until it is here, the keyboard asks for it on its own the first time you dictate with a model downloaded, and until it lands dictation says so instead of recording. Direct-download builds carry it from the start.

The F-Droid build fetches whisper.cpp's library the first time too, but only after you turn on **Settings › Privacy › Feature libraries**, which F-Droid requires apps to ask for. Until then the Whisper screen shows a banner that turns it on, and dictating from the keyboard says the downloads are off rather than that something is downloading. The offline build can't download it at all, so the keyboard points you to the import on the same screen.

The catalog has 10 whisper.cpp models: Tiny, Base, Small, Medium, Turbo and Large v3, which take any language, and English-only Tiny, Base, Small and Medium, which show once English is enabled. Tiny to Small are 8-bit, which loses nothing measurable; Medium and up are 5-bit, which keeps the downloads to 540 MB for Medium, 574 MB for Turbo and 1.1 GB for Large v3. Beside them sit 29 LiteRT models. Ten are multilingual graphs: seven auto-detect the spoken language, and three are grouped models you can pin to one language. The other 19 are single-language models covering 13 languages, several at more than one size: English, German, Spanish, French, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese, Russian, Tamil, Telugu, Urdu, and Chinese. A single-language model only appears in the catalog once you've enabled that language on the Languages screen.

<Shot id="tools/whisper-catalog" alt="Model catalog with size and language filters, grouped by tier." />

The screen is four sections in a row: the per-language routing list, then **Your voice models**, then a short **Suggested for your languages** shortlist, then a collapsed browse section holding everything else. Browse carries one filter, a chip per size, and it splits what's left into the models that take any language and the ones locked to a single one. Each row wears its size, how many languages it covers, and its download size as small chips. Small is the only model carrying a Recommended badge: the whisper.cpp one where it runs, else the LiteRT one.

Turbo and Large v3 need a 128-band mel spectrogram, where every older Whisper model needs 80. Each one downloads a different filterbank file alongside its graph, and the audio pipeline reads the band count from that file. If the two files ever disagree, dictation fails with a message instead of decoding noise into confident-looking words.

### When a language can only be guessed at

With a whisper.cpp model nothing has to be guessed: it is told the language you dictate in, whichever of Whisper's 99 it is. The rest of this section is about the LiteRT models, and only matters if you use one.

Every LiteRT model has to decide which language you're speaking, unless it was built for one language or belongs to a grouped model that takes the language as an input. The grouped models list 40 and 26 languages, and those lists are compiled into the graph itself, so a language outside them cannot be forced no matter what the app asks for.

For those languages, and Bangla is one of them, a model can decide you're speaking a bigger neighbouring language and hand back fluent sentences in the wrong one. Nothing errors, because as far as the model is concerned it did its job. Two things help:

- The model-per-language list says so on any affected language, and points at Turbo and Large v3, which get the language right far more often than the small models do.
- When the wrong language happens to be written in a different alphabet, WM Keyboard converts it back. Bangla read as Hindi is the common case: the model still transcribed the Bangla words it heard, it just spelled them in Devanagari, and the two alphabets line up closely enough to map across. You get readable Bangla instead of a line you can't read at all. It can't rescue a wrong language, only a wrong alphabet.

Whisper itself has an "initial prompt" that biases spelling and vocabulary, and its own way of forcing a language. The whisper.cpp models use both. The LiteRT models can't: the community `.tflite` conversions were exported with the entire generation loop traced inside the graph. That leaves two inputs: the spectrogram, and on a grouped model a number selecting one of the languages it was built with.

### Picking a model per language

<PhoneFrame caption="Model-per-language routing list, with a coverage warning on one row.">
![Model-per-language routing list, with a coverage warning on one row.](@assets/screens/tools/whisper-routing.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

Rather than one global model for every language, WM Keyboard resolves a model per enabled language and falls back in this order:

1. A model you've explicitly pinned to that language, if it's downloaded and covers it.
2. The largest downloaded single-language model for that language.
3. The model that **Model for other languages** names, if it's downloaded and covers the language. See [Options](#options).
4. The best-ranked downloaded model that covers the language at all.
5. As a last resort, that same fallback or the first downloaded model, even if it can't actually transcribe that language.

That last step is deliberate: it produces wrong words rather than a dead microphone. When a language's assigned model can't cover it, the row is flagged in the error color with a note to download one that does.

## Downloading and managing models

<PhoneFrame side caption="A model mid-download, with progress and the storage summary below the list.">
![A model mid-download, with progress and the storage summary below the list.](@assets/screens/tools/whisper-download-progress.webp)
</PhoneFrame>

Each catalog row carries its own **Download** button on the right. Tapping the row itself does something else. It expands the row to show what the model is and which languages it covers, so you can read up before spending the bandwidth. Once a model is on disk, that button becomes a delete icon. A failed attempt offers **Retry**, and an interrupted one offers **Resume**.

A whisper.cpp model downloads as one `.bin` file from a fixed revision of the whisper.cpp project's repository, and is checked against its published SHA-256 before it counts as downloaded; a damaged file is deleted and the row says so. A LiteRT model downloads as two files (the `.tflite` graph and a companion vocabulary/filterbank file), with the smaller vocabulary file fetched first so a broken connection fails fast. Downloads are resumable: if you lose connection or leave the app, the next attempt picks up where it left off instead of restarting.

Only one model downloads at a time. Before it starts, WM Keyboard checks that you have enough free space: the remaining bytes plus a safety margin. If you don't, the download stops with a message telling you roughly how much to free up. On a metered connection, any model at or above roughly 150 MB asks you to confirm before it uses mobile data.

Downloaded models live in the app's private storage, not shared storage: the screen shows total space used and lets you delete a model to reclaim it. Deleting a model also clears any per-language pin that pointed at it, and clears the app's own fallback if that was the deleted model. If an app update ever drops a model from the catalog, leftover files show up as reclaimable space with a one-tap "Free up" button.

If you downloaded **Tiny (English)** in version 0.5.8 or earlier and dictation with it came back empty most of the time, that was the model file, not your microphone. Its source has since changed to a conversion that works, and the old file is recognised and discarded, so the model shows as not downloaded until you download it again.

The English-only LiteRT models (Tiny, Base, Small and Medium, each marked English) had a subtler fault of their own. Their conversion told the model to start every phrase from the wrong prompt, so a short phrase often came back empty, and more often still with other people talking nearby. The keyboard now corrects that inside the file the first time each one is used, so there's nothing to download again. The first phrase after the fix takes a moment longer while it happens. On recorded test speech it took Tiny (English) from 12 to 9 wrong words in a hundred, and from five empty results in sixteen short phrases to none.

A phone that cannot download can import a model instead. Below the catalog, **Get the files** lists both files of each model that suits your languages, to fetch in a browser on any device, and **Import** installs them: pick the `.tflite` and its `filters_vocab_*.bin` together, or one after the other in either order. A model imported without its vocabulary file is reported as still needing it. See [Using it without internet](/privacy/offline/#voice-models).

### Your own whisper.cpp models

**Import** also takes a whisper.cpp model that isn't in the catalog, such as one fine-tuned for a language or a dialect and converted to whisper.cpp's `ggml-*.bin` format. The keyboard reads the start of the file to check that it's a whisper.cpp Whisper model and doesn't care what it's called; a GGUF file, or any other kind of model, is turned away. It then lists the model under **Your models** by its file name, with a size worked out from its layers, and runs it on whisper.cpp like the catalog's own. A model built on Whisper's English-only vocabulary counts as English-only; any other is offered for every language.

The keyboard can't tell which languages a fine-tune was trained for, so with other models on the phone it picks one per language by the usual rules and the fine-tune may not be the one it lands on. Open **Model per language** and pick it for the languages it was made for: a Hinglish model under Hindi or Hinglish, for example. Its row has no translate option, since a fine-tune may not have kept that task. Importing a file with the same name again replaces the earlier copy, and deleting the model removes it for good, since there's nothing to download it from again. Builds without whisper.cpp can't import one.

When exactly one model is on disk, the app quietly adopts it as that fallback, so dictation works right after your first download with no setup step in between. It does the same again if you delete whichever model was holding the job.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Voice typing" />

The **Recognition engine** list on the **Voice typing** screen is where you add Whisper; **Add a provider** offers it in any build wherever whisper.cpp or LiteRT can run. Move it to the top to make it the engine for every language. Press its row to open Whisper's own screen, which holds the rest of the table: **Translate to English**, **Compute** and the model catalog.

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition engine | System recognizer | Switches dictation between the system speech recognizer and offline Whisper. On the F-Droid build it offers the whisper.cpp models only, since that build has no LiteRT. |
| Translate to English | Off | Speaks any language and types its English translation instead of a transcription, when the routed model supports it. |
| Model per language | Automatic | Pins a specific downloaded model to a language, overriding automatic routing. One row per enabled language, each with a **Change** button. |
| Model for other languages | Automatic | The fallback step 3 of the routing order uses. Only shown once more than one model is on disk. |
| Compute | Automatic | Where the whisper.cpp models run: **Automatic**, **CPU** or **GPU**. Only shown once a whisper.cpp model is on disk. See [Running on the GPU](#running-on-the-gpu). |

**Model for other languages** sets that fallback by hand. It sits under **Your models**, below the per-language list, and it only appears once you have more than one model on disk, where the choice means something. **Automatic** is the default and keeps the old behaviour: the best downloaded model that covers the language you're typing in. Pick a model instead and every unpinned language uses that one.

Dictation still resolves per language, from the language of the active layout, the same way the system recognizer follows the layout's locale. A [Voice typing language](/tools/voice/#language-and-engine) you picked takes the layout's place. With several picked, the model for the first is used and left to detect which one you speak, so pick a multilingual model that covers them all. The app also fills the fallback in for you when a model is the only one on disk, and clears the choice if you delete that model.

Nine of the 10 multilingual models support translation. Turbo doesn't, because large-v3-turbo was trained for transcription only, and none of the 19 single-language models do either. If a language routes to one of those, the translate toggle has no visible effect for it.

### Running on the GPU

**Compute** decides where the whisper.cpp models run. The LiteRT models always run on the processor (CPU), whatever it says.

- **Automatic** *(default)*: the GPU for Base and larger models on a phone with a Snapdragon chip (an Adreno GPU). Tiny stays on the CPU, which reads a phrase about as fast as the GPU takes to get going. Other GPUs are used only when you pick **GPU**.
- **CPU**: always the processor.
- **GPU**: the GPU for every size, Tiny included.

On Qualcomm's Adreno, the GPU in Snapdragon phones, whisper.cpp runs through the OpenCL driver, and its GPU code comes with the engines. Other GPUs (Mali, Xclipse, PowerVR) run it through Vulkan, from a build of whisper.cpp that is a separate download of about 12 MB (38 MB on the phone), fetched when you pick **GPU**: it carries every shader compiled in. On many of those GPUs it is no faster than the CPU, which is why **Automatic** leaves them out. A phone with neither runs on the CPU whatever you pick; the row's subtitle says so. The GPU is quickest at the part of the work that reads the sound (the encoder), which is most of the wait with Small and the bigger models, and it frees the processor while it runs. The words come out the same, give or take a comma in a rare phrase, since the GPU does its sums at a different precision.

The first dictation on the GPU after an install or an update can take some seconds longer while the GPU prepares itself; the app keeps what it prepared, so later ones start at once. If the GPU won't start, has no room for the model, or stops in the middle of a phrase, that phrase goes to the CPU and comes out as usual, and from then on the CPU runs that model on this phone. The subtitle says when that has happened. Pick **GPU** again to give it another try; an update of the app or of Android does the same.

## Details & edge cases

**A whisper.cpp clip runs to two minutes.** whisper.cpp moves Whisper's 30-second window along a longer clip, so its models record for up to two minutes before a clip ends by itself, with the same countdown over the last five seconds when dictation stops there. A transcription works through it window by window, and the words appear under the microphone as they come.

**A LiteRT clip is a fixed 30-second window.** Whisper always processes a full 30-second spectrogram: a short utterance is padded, and hitting the 30-second mark auto-triggers transcription of whatever was captured. With **Keep listening** off, anything you say after that mark isn't recorded, so for the last five seconds the line under the microphone counts down ("Stops in 3 seconds") instead of saying "Listening", and it turns to "Transcribing…" the moment recording ends. The model's own work is therefore roughly constant per phrase rather than scaling with how long you spoke. The preparation in front of it isn't: the padding is silence, whose spectrogram is known without computing it, so only the part you spoke is analysed. The model also loads while you're still talking instead of after you stop, so the first phrase of a session doesn't wait for it.

**Long dictation keeps every word.** With **Keep listening** on, the microphone never closes between clips. When one ends by itself, at the 30-second mark (two minutes on whisper.cpp), after a pause, or at a key on the panel, the next clip starts recording from the very next moment while the last one is transcribed, so you can keep talking through "Transcribing…" and nothing you say in that gap is lost. Its words land after the last clip's, in order. In a clip's last three seconds a short pause between words ends it early, so the cut falls between two words rather than through one, and there is no countdown because nothing is cut off. The microphone indicator stays on the whole time. Pressing stop, typing on the keyboard or closing the voice surface still closes the microphone at once. Engines that turn speech into text as you talk, [asr4all](/tools/asr4all/), [Sherpa](/tools/sherpa/) and [Omnilingual](/tools/omnilingual/), keep their own clip handling.

**A silent clip types nothing.** Whisper has no way to answer "I heard nothing". Hand it a silent window and it writes the likeliest caption for one, which after training on subtitled video is "Thank you." or "Thanks for watching!". So a clip shorter than 0.3 seconds, or one whose loudest moment is no louder than a quiet room, is never transcribed at all. A clip that is faint but not silent is transcribed, and if all that comes back is one of those stock phrases it isn't typed. Say "thank you" at speaking volume and it's typed like anything else. A transcript that is only a sound caption, such as `[Music]` or `(coughs)`, is never typed, and a word or short phrase the decoder repeats five or more times in a row is folded back to one. The [transcription server](/tools/transcription-server/) gets the same treatment, which also saves the upload.

**A pause can end the clip.** Set **Stop after a pause** under Voice typing (off by default, 0.5 to 4 seconds) and the clip ends by itself once you have said something and then gone quiet for that long, instead of waiting for a press or the 30-second mark. The quiet before you start talking never counts. With **Keep listening** off that ends dictation too, so the whole exchange is hands-free; with it on, the next clip starts at once on the same microphone, as described below.

**An open microphone winds down.** With **Keep listening** on, a full 30-second clip with nothing in it starts the next one quietly, but only twice in a row; after that dictation goes idle. The [type-while-you-speak modes](/tools/voice/#typing-while-you-dictate) allow twelve, as they do for the system recognizer.

**A microphone that drops out keeps what it had.** If capture fails in the middle of a clip, because another app tore it down or the audio service restarted, what you said up to that point is transcribed as though you had pressed stop. If it fails before anything was recorded, the panel says the microphone couldn't be opened.

**Only three rows here can be reset.** **Translate to English**, **Model for other languages** and **Compute** are ordinary settings, so each grows the usual restore button once you move it off the default. The **Recognition engine** list has a **Reset to default** row of its own once you change it. See [Putting one setting back](/reference/settings/#putting-one-setting-back). The per-language routing rows and the catalog aren't settings rows at all, so they have no button. You undo a pin by opening that language's dialog and picking **Automatic** again.

**Speed has no benchmarked numbers.** The app carries no measured per-device timing table, for the CPU or the GPU. The only official guidance is the relative sizing above, with Tiny fastest and the two Large graphs slowest and heaviest on RAM, plus a general note that bigger models are more accurate and slower to download and to transcribe.

**Every build, different catalogs.** The whisper.cpp models run in every build, F-Droid's included. The LiteRT models need the LiteRT runtime, which the F-Droid build doesn't carry, so its catalog lists the whisper.cpp models alone. A build made without the whisper.cpp library offers no Whisper engine at all. See [Which build you have](/start/editions/).

**Power saving can silently override your choice.** Its "On-device models" option is on by default. Power saving never turns on by itself unless you set **Turn on by itself**, but once it's on, by hand or by a trigger you chose, dictation falls back to the system recognizer even with Whisper selected and a model downloaded. If dictation seems to have switched engines on its own, check your power-saving settings first.

**No mic opens without a model.** With Whisper selected and nothing downloaded, the panel says so and offers both ways forward rather than opening the microphone. One chip opens this screen so you can download a model. The other switches back to the system recognizer and starts listening right away, and that switch is saved, the same as changing the engine in settings. The compact bar has room for one line, so it points you at settings, where both choices live.

**Panel keys don't end a recording.** Space, backspace and enter on the voice panel's right-hand rail edit the text while the microphone stays open, so you can add a space or fix a character without losing what you've said. A keystroke that lands while a clip is being transcribed behaves the same way. The audio is already captured, so the words still arrive. They just land after whatever you typed. Typing on the keyboard itself during a system-recognizer dictation still ends that utterance, because its live partial text sits in the editor and a keystroke inside it would corrupt it.

**Privacy and downloads are separate concerns.** Transcription itself makes no network calls. The only network activity Whisper causes is fetching a model file over HTTPS from its public repository when you tap download (see [Network policy](/privacy/network/) for the full picture of what WM Keyboard can send and when).

**Attribution.** The whisper.cpp models run on whisper.cpp and ggml (MIT, the ggml authors), and are the whisper.cpp project's quantized conversions of OpenAI's weights. The LiteRT models run on TensorFlow Lite, branded LiteRT (Apache-2.0, Google). The model architecture and weights are OpenAI's Whisper (MIT). The `.tflite` graphs themselves are third-party conversions hosted on Hugging Face, not built by this project. Full credits are in the app's About screen.
