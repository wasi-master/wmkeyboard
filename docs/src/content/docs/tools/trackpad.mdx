Placing the cursor by tapping the text is the least precise thing a touchscreen asks of you. The **Trackpad** tool takes the key area over and turns it into a laptop-style pad: put a finger down anywhere and the cursor moves by how far you drag, a character at a time sideways and a line at a time up or down. Hold first and the same drag selects. It is the same idea as the [spacebar cursor swipe](/typing/gestures/), with the whole keyboard to move across instead of one key.

## Opening it

Tap **Trackpad** on the toolbar, or in the toolbox if you haven't pinned it (see [the toolbar](/tools/overview/)). The tool lights up while the panel is open, and tapping it again, or the <KeyCap>abc</KeyCap> key on the panel's bottom row, puts the keys back.

**Press and hold** the tool instead and the trackpad is open only for as long as your finger stays down. Drag on it with your other thumb, let go of the tool, and you are back on the keys. This is the one for a quick nudge in the middle of typing. A hold over a trackpad you already opened with a tap does nothing, so that panel stays put when you let go.

## Gestures

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| One finger drag | Move the cursor. Sideways is characters, up and down is lines |
| Hold, then drag | Select. Selection mode is on until you lift the finger |
| Two taps | Select the word at the cursor |
| Three taps | Select the whole line at the cursor |
| Two finger drag | Move the cursor a word at a time |
| Two finger tap | Type a space |

The pad is relative. Where you put your finger down doesn't matter, only how far it travels from there, so you can start a drag anywhere on the surface, lift, and start again. A slow drag that covers less than a step per frame still adds up: the surface keeps the fraction and fires the step once the travel reaches it, and dragging back the way you came unwinds that fraction rather than firing a move.

Every move is a real arrow key sent to the field, the same event a hardware keyboard would send, through the same handler the [text-editing panel](/tools/text-editing/) uses. That has two consequences. The cursor behaves exactly as the app you're typing in wants it to, and the trackpad obeys whatever selection mode is armed, from wherever it was armed. Turn on the toolbar's [Selection mode](/tools/text-editing/#selection-mode) tool, or the panel's <KeyCap>Select</KeyCap> key, and every trackpad drag extends the selection until you turn it off again.

**Hold, then drag** is the short form of that. The hold arms selection mode, the drag extends the selection, and lifting the finger disarms the mode. What you selected stays selected: the selection lives in the editor and nothing on the keyboard collapses it. Move the cursor again with a plain drag and it collapses, the way an arrow key collapses a selection anywhere else. A word or line picked up by two or three taps also stays until the next plain move, so tap twice and then hold-drag to grow the selection from the word outward.

**Two fingers** move by words: Ctrl+Arrow to the field, in the direction you drag. Both fingers down and up together with no travel is a space, so you can separate words without leaving the pad. A third finger counts as two.

## Mouse mode

Mouse mode is off until you turn it on: <SettingsPath path="Tools / Trackpad / Mouse mode" />. Once it's on, a chip in the trackpad's top corner switches it between **Cursor**, everything above, and **Mouse**. In mouse mode an arrow pointer appears over the app above the keyboard and the surface drives it the way a laptop's pad drives its pointer: you can press buttons, open links, scroll lists and drag things in the app without reaching up to touch them. The panel remembers which mode you left it in, so the next time you open the trackpad it comes back the same way. Tap the chip again, or the <KeyCap>abc</KeyCap> key, to go back.

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| One finger drag | Move the pointer |
| Tap | Click |
| Two quick taps | Double click |
| Two finger tap | Right click |
| Two finger drag | Scroll |
| Hold, then drag | Drag and drop. The button stays down until you lift the finger |

The pointer stays in the part of the screen the app has above the keyboard, since a click on the keys themselves would only type. A quick flick carries it further than a slow drag of the same length, so one swipe crosses the screen and a careful drag still lands on a small button; [Options](#options) has the speed and a switch for that.

### How clicks reach the app

Android doesn't let one app click in another on its own, so mouse mode works through one of two routes, and the tool's settings page says which one is in use.

- **Shizuku.** Turn on **Use Shizuku or root** in the Mouse mode settings (the same switch as [Shizuku or root for keyboard features](/reference/settings/permissions/#shizuku-or-root)) and allow the keyboard from the **Shizuku** row under it, which also helps you get or start Shizuku. With the keyboard allowed (Sui counts too), clicks go in as events from a real mouse. Apps treat them exactly as they would a USB or Bluetooth mouse: a right click opens the context menu where an app has one, scrolling is a mouse wheel, and links and buttons see the pointer hover over them.
- **The touch mouse service.** Without Shizuku, the keyboard uses an accessibility service of its own, **WM Keyboard touch mouse**, which you turn on once. It touches the screen where the pointer is: a click is a tap, a right click is a long press, a scroll is a slow swipe that stops before it lifts so the page doesn't fling, and a drag is a held touch that starts with a long press, which is what launchers and lists wait for before they pick an item up. The service reads nothing on screen and does nothing unless you're clicking on the trackpad. On Android 7 a drag or a scroll is played back as one swipe when you lift your fingers, because that version can't keep a touch going while it moves.

With neither set up, the pointer still moves, and a line on the surface offers to open the touch mouse service's explanation and then Android's accessibility settings.

Root without Shizuku or Sui uses the touch mouse service. The Google Play build has neither route, so it has no mouse mode and the chip doesn't appear.

### When mouse mode isn't offered

- On the lock screen. A keyboard there must not click around behind it, so the chip is hidden until you unlock.
- With the keyboard floating, or in the full-screen text box some apps use in landscape. The pointer needs the strip of app above a docked keyboard to live in.
- When the app leaves less than about a finger's width of screen above the keyboard.

Clicking something that takes the focus away from the text box you're typing in closes the keyboard, and the trackpad with it, as it would if you'd tapped there. Turn on [Keep on screen](/typing/size-position/#keeping-the-keyboard-on-screen) to have the keyboard come straight back.

## Visual feedback

The tool's button is lit on the toolbar while the panel is open, however it was opened. On the surface, a trail follows your finger and a crosshair marks where it is now; both go the moment you lift. The trail can be turned off under [Options](#options). A faint hint in the middle of the surface fades while you are touching it.

While you drag, a **magnifier** shows the line of text around the cursor, enlarged, in a bubble over the text, with the cursor in its middle. It comes up when a drag starts or a hold arms selection, and it follows the cursor from line to line. Any selection shows highlighted in it, and the cursor in it is the end of the selection that is moving. A plain tap never shows it. The bubble is the keyboard's own drawing of the text the app hands it, not a zoomed picture of the screen, so it uses the keyboard's colours and font. It sits above the cursor, or below it when the cursor is too close to the top of the screen, for apps that tell the keyboard where their cursor is, which most do. For an app that doesn't, or when the cursor is scrolled out of sight or hidden behind the keyboard, it sits just above the keyboard instead. In a password field it shows dots, as the field does, never the password itself. With a long selection (more than about a thousand characters, such as after Select all) it shows the text past the moving end, with the selected side tinted, rather than reading the whole selection back on every step. It never takes a touch and it goes the moment you lift your finger.

## The panel is a layout

The trackpad is a [panel layout](/languages/custom-layouts/#panel-layouts) like the emoji, clipboard and text-editing panels. Out of the box it is the pointing surface over an <KeyCap>abc</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>space</KeyCap> and <KeyCap>⌫</KeyCap> row. Open the layout editor from the tool's page (or from **Panel layouts** in the layouts gallery) to change the row, add keys beside the surface, give the surface more or less height, or put a text-editing key on it. The surface itself is the one component the layout cannot lose: a layout without it gets one added back.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / Trackpad / Options" />

**Horizontal sensitivity**: default **12 dp**, adjustable from 4 to 48. How far your finger travels for each character. Lower is faster.

**Vertical sensitivity**: default **28 dp**, adjustable from 8 to 96. How far your finger travels for each line. It starts coarser than the horizontal step on purpose: a line is a bigger jump than a character, and a finger dragging sideways drifts up and down more than it means to. Two fingers move a word for every two horizontal steps of travel.

**Press and hold to open**: on by default. Holding the tool on the toolbar opens the trackpad until you let go. Turn it off and a hold there opens the tool's settings page instead, the way a hold on any other toolbar tool does, or runs whatever second action you bound to it. Dragging the tool to move it works either way: once your finger travels far enough to read as a drag, the panel closes and the tool picks itself up as usual. While the toolbox is open the hold does not open the trackpad at all, so the toolbox stays on screen and you can drag the tool off the toolbar and back into the grid. A still hold there opens the tool's settings page.

**Two taps select a word**: on by default. Two quick taps on the surface select the word at the cursor and three select the line, on the system's double-tap window and within a thumb's width of each other. Turn it off if your drags tend to start with a tap you didn't mean.

**Vibrate on each move**: on by default. A short tick for every character or line the cursor moves.

**Show the trail**: on by default. Draws your finger's path and a crosshair while you drag.

**Magnifier**: on by default. Shows the text at the cursor, enlarged, while you drag (see [Visual feedback](#visual-feedback)). Turn off **Follow the text** under the spacebar's **Cursor magnifier** and it stays still above the keyboard instead of moving with the cursor. The [spacebar cursor swipe](/typing/gestures/#moving-the-cursor-with-a-spacebar-swipe) has a separate switch for the same bubble.

**Panel layout** opens the layout editor described above.

### Mouse mode

Not in the Google Play build.

**Mouse mode**: off by default. Puts the Cursor / Mouse chip on the trackpad. The rows below show once it's on.

**How clicks reach the app**: which [route](#how-clicks-reach-the-app) the mouse is using now. While neither route is set up, a tap explains the touch mouse service and then opens Android's accessibility settings; with the service on, a tap goes straight to those settings, where it's turned off again.

**Use Shizuku or root**: the keyboard's Shizuku switch, the same one as on the permissions page. When it's on, the **Shizuku** row under it says what's missing and takes you there: getting Shizuku, starting it, or allowing the keyboard in it.

**Pointer speed**: default **100%**, adjustable from 25% to 400%. How far the pointer moves for each drag on the trackpad.

**Pointer acceleration**: on by default. A quick flick moves the pointer further than a slow drag of the same length.

**Scroll speed**: default **100%**, adjustable from 25% to 400%. How far a two-finger drag scrolls.

**Natural scrolling**: on by default. Two fingers move the page the way a finger on the screen would: drag up and the page goes up. Turn it off to scroll the other way, like a mouse wheel.

## Details and edge cases

- The trackpad is a panel, so opening it closes whatever panel was open and puts down any pending shift, Ctrl or Alt.
- The bottom row's <KeyCap>space</KeyCap> is an ordinary spacebar, swipes included, so the language switch and the spacebar cursor swipe still work from inside the panel.
- The panel never goes full-bleed, because the toolbar has to stay for the hold gesture that may have opened it.
- With a hardware keyboard, the panel has no focus ring: it is a pointing surface with nothing to arrow between, like the handwriting canvas. Bind a leader letter to open it under <SettingsPath path="Typing / Physical keyboard" />; the tool ships without one because every letter is taken.
- The trackpad works before the first unlock after a restart, since in cursor mode it only touches the field it is typing into. Mouse mode is hidden on the lock screen.
- The mouse pointer is drawn by the keyboard, so it goes away whenever the keyboard does, and a held drag lets go if the keyboard closes under it.
- If the keyboard closes while you are holding the tool, the hold ends with it; there is no release to wait for.
