[KDE Connect](https://kdeconnect.kde.org/) links a phone to a computer over your home network. The **KDE Connect** tool speaks that protocol from inside the keyboard, so you can reach your computer from whatever app you are typing in, and the computer can type back. It works with KDE Connect on Linux, Windows and macOS, with GSConnect on GNOME, and with Valent.

You do not need the KDE Connect app on the phone. The keyboard shows up on the computer as a device of its own, named after the phone's model followed by **WM Keyboard**, for example *Pixel 8 - WM Keyboard*. If you do have the app, both keep working side by side, as two devices, and the name tells you which is which.

To move a file to another phone, or to anyone as a link, see the [Send files](/tools/send/) tool: its Nearby mode runs on this same engine, and its direct link joins two phones with no Wi-Fi between them.

## Turning it on

**Use KDE Connect** is on from the start, but being on sends nothing by itself. The keyboard only goes on your network once a computer is paired, or while a device list is open in the panel or in settings. Turn the switch off under <SettingsPath path="Tools / KDE Connect" /> and it does nothing at all on your network: it sends nothing and listens for nothing.

After that the panel lists the computers it can see. On the computer, KDE Connect (or GSConnect, or Valent) has to be running.

## Pairing

1. Press **Pair** next to the computer's name.
2. An eight character code appears on the phone. The same code appears on the computer.
3. If the two match, accept on the computer. You have 30 seconds.

You can also start from the computer: pick the phone there, press pair, and accept on the phone. That only works while the keyboard's device list is open, because that is the only time the keyboard talks to computers it has not paired with.

The code is what makes pairing safe. It is calculated from both devices' keys, so a match means the computer on your screen is the one in front of you and not something else on the Wi-Fi. After pairing, the computer's certificate is remembered and nothing else is accepted under its name.

### Pairing with WM Keyboard on a computer

WMK Desktop, WM Keyboard for the Mac, shows a pairing code under **Backup & restore › Sync devices › Pair a phone**, and on its setup page. Point the phone's camera, or any QR scanner, at the code and open the link it shows, and WM Keyboard opens on the devices screen. Or press **Scan a code** in this panel, next to **Add by address**, which opens the keyboard's own [QR scanner](/tools/scanner/) (not in the F-Droid build). A link opened from the camera or another app first asks whether to pair, naming the computer and its address, because any web page or app can open such a link; press **Pair** only for a code you scanned from your own computer. The keyboard's scanner pairs at once. Either way there is no code to compare. The link holds the computer's address and its certificate fingerprint, so the phone pairs only with the computer that showed it, and its one-time token works once.

Once paired, the two sync through each other: see [Sync between devices](/privacy/backup/#sync-between-devices).

### If the computer does not show up

- Both devices have to be on the same network. Mobile data does not work, and many guest networks keep devices apart.
- The computer's firewall has to allow ports **1714 to 1764**, for both TCP and UDP.
- Press **Add by address** and type the computer's IP address. The phone then announces itself to that address directly, which gets through on networks that block discovery.
- If another KDE Connect app on the phone is already using the discovery port, the keyboard can still be found. Press **Refresh** on the phone rather than waiting for the computer.

## The tabs

Tap the device name in the panel's header at any time to switch computers, pair another one, or unpair.

Swipe sideways across the panel to move between tabs. On the touchpads a swipe moves the pointer instead, so start it off the pad.

Not every computer can do everything. A tab or button for something the computer does not support is left out, or says so when you open it.

### Input

The big area is a **touchpad** for the computer.

| Gesture | What it does |
|---|---|
| One finger drag | Move the pointer |
| Tap | Left click. Tap twice for a double click |
| Two finger tap | Right click |
| Three finger tap | Middle click |
| Two finger drag | Scroll |
| Hold still, then drag | Drag with the button held. Lift to drop |
| Tap, then drag straight away | The same drag, without the wait |

Slow, careful movement moves the pointer by single pixels rather than not at all, and a steady drag keeps a steady pointer speed, so the pointer glides rather than stepping.

The <KeyCap>L</KeyCap> <KeyCap>M</KeyCap> <KeyCap>R</KeyCap> buttons beside it are the mouse buttons, for when a tap is not precise enough.

Press **Type on computer** and the keys come back under a smaller panel. Now everything you type goes to the computer instead of the app: letters, glide typing, suggestions you pick, autocorrections, emoji, and any language the keyboard has. A strip of keys a phone layout does not have sits on top: <KeyCap>Esc</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>Tab</KeyCap>, the arrows, <KeyCap>Del</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>Home</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>End</KeyCap>, page keys and <KeyCap>F1</KeyCap> to <KeyCap>F12</KeyCap>. <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>Alt</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>Super</KeyCap> and <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap> stay lit until the next key, so <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap> then <KeyCap>c</KeyCap> copies on the computer.

**Compose** changes how typing is sent. Normally each keystroke goes out as you make it. With Compose on you write a whole line on the phone, fix it, and send it with <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap>. The **Send** button sends the line without pressing Enter on the computer.

### Media

Whatever is playing on the computer: artwork, title, a seek bar, previous, play or pause, next. Shuffle and repeat appear for players that have them. If several players are open you can switch between them. The slider at the bottom is the computer's own volume, with a mute button.

KDE Connect on macOS does not let a phone control its players, so on a Mac this tab has only the volume. The official KDE Connect app has the same limit there. A [Run](#run) command such as `osascript -e 'tell application "Music" to playpause'` is a way around it.

### Send

Until something has been sent or received, the three buttons fill the tab as large tiles. Once there are transfers they shrink to a row above the list.

- **Clipboard** sends what is on the phone's clipboard right now.
- **Text in the field** sends your selection, or the whole field if nothing is selected.
- **Files…** opens the keyboard's [file manager](/tools/files/) (Android's picker in the Play Store build) and sends what you choose.
- A place goes from the [Location tool](/tools/location/#sending-a-place-to-your-computer) instead: its **Send to** button sends it as a map link, which the computer opens in its browser.

Transfers are listed with their progress and can be cancelled. Files the computer sends to the phone are listed here too, with an **Open** button.

### Run

The commands you have set up on the computer, as buttons. Output shows up live while a command runs, and **Stop** ends it. Commands are created on the computer, in its KDE Connect settings. **Set up on computer** opens that screen there.

### Slides

Big **previous** and **next** buttons for a presentation, **Start** (F5) and **End** (Esc). Hold and drag on the pad between them to show the computer's pointer dot and move it around. Let go and it disappears.

### Device

**Ping** the computer, **Ring it** to find a laptop in a bag, and **Lock** or **Unlock** its session where the desktop supports it. The device's address, battery and certificate fingerprint are here, along with **Unpair**, and **Pause clipboard**: see [Pausing clipboard sharing](#pausing-clipboard-sharing).

## What the computer can do

- **Type into the phone.** On the computer, open the phone in KDE Connect and use its remote keyboard box. What you type lands in the field on the phone. Arrows, <KeyCap>Home</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>End</KeyCap>, selection with <KeyCap>Shift</KeyCap>, word moves with <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>, <KeyCap>Ctrl</KeyCap>+<KeyCap>A</KeyCap> <KeyCap>C</KeyCap> <KeyCap>X</KeyCap> <KeyCap>V</KeyCap> <KeyCap>Z</KeyCap>, and <KeyCap>Enter</KeyCap> all do what you expect. This works only while the keyboard is on screen and the phone is unlocked.
- **Share its clipboard.** Text copied on the computer is ready to paste on the phone, and shows up as the paste suggestion.
- **Send text, links and files.** Text and links arrive on the clipboard. Files are saved to **Downloads/WM Keyboard** and added to the [clipboard history](/tools/clipboard/), so you can paste a picture from the computer straight into a chat.
- **See the phone's battery**, and **control the phone's media** if you allow it.

## Pausing clipboard sharing

Sometimes you want the clipboard to stay on one side for a while, say while you copy passwords on the computer or show your screen to someone, without unpairing and without flipping the clipboard switches one by one.

In the panel, open the **Device** tab and press **Pause clipboard**. Pick how long: **15 min**, **1 hour**, or **Until I resume**. Tick **All computers** first to pause every paired computer at once instead of only the one you are on. The same choice is on the **Devices** screen under <SettingsPath path="Tools / KDE Connect / Devices" />: a **Pause clipboard** button under each computer, and an **All computers** row at the top when more than one is paired.

While a computer is paused, nothing you copy on the phone goes to it, and nothing it copies lands on the phone. Everything else keeps working: typing, the touchpad, media, files. A timed pause ends by itself, and a pause outlasts a restart of the phone or the keyboard. To end it sooner, press **Resume clipboard** in the panel, **Resume** on the Devices screen, or **Resume** on the notification that shows while a pause is on. A computer held by a pause on all of them is resumed together with the rest.

**Send › Clipboard** still sends while paused, because pressing it is asking for that one clip to go. Pauses are kept on this phone only and never sync to another device.

## Options

<SettingsPath path="Tools / KDE Connect" />

KDE Connect is the **KDE Connect** bundle on [Extensions](/reference/settings/extensions/). Off there, the tool, the devices screen and the pairing leave the keyboard and the app; the pairing itself is kept until you switch it back on.

**Use KDE Connect**: on by default. The master switch. Nothing goes on the network until a computer is paired or a device list is open.

**Devices**: paired computers and the ones nearby, with a switch per computer for clipboard, typing, files, phone media, battery and sync, and a [pause for the clipboard](#pausing-clipboard-sharing). Also the list of addresses added by hand, and this phone's certificate fingerprint.

**Name shown on the computer**: blank uses the phone's model followed by *WM Keyboard*, shortened to *WMK* when the model name is long. Up to 32 characters, and the protocol does not allow punctuation.

**Stay connected**: default **Keyboard showing**.
- *Panel open*: only while the KDE Connect panel is open.
- *Keyboard showing*: while the keyboard is on screen and for a minute after, so switching apps does not reconnect every time.
- *In the background too* (called *Always* before 0.6.4): the link also stays up after the keyboard hides, so the clipboard can keep syncing while you are not typing. There is no background service behind it. The link lives inside the keyboard, and once the keyboard is off screen Android is free to pause or close it, which many phones do within minutes and some at once; the link drops then, and comes back the next time the keyboard shows. Setting WM Keyboard's battery use to *Unrestricted* in Android's app settings keeps it up longer on most phones, but nothing guarantees it. Power saving, with its *Background network* switch on, puts it back to *Keyboard showing*.

**Connect automatically**: default on. Reconnect to paired computers when they are found.

**Receive the computer's clipboard** and **Send this phone's clipboard**: both default on.

**Clear received passwords**: default **1 minute**, up to 30, or *Never*. Text from the computer that looks like a password or a one-time code is taken off the phone's clipboard after this long, along with its unpinned copy in clipboard history. It is only cleared if it is still the latest clip, so something you copied since stays. Ordinary text from the computer is never cleared.

**Let the computer type here**: default on.

**Use the keyboard language**: default off. Off, what the computer sends is typed exactly. On, its keys go through the keyboard's layout the way a [hardware keyboard's](/typing/hardware-keyboard/) do, so a Latin keyboard on your desk can write Bengali through Avro, with suggestions and autocorrect.

**Pointer speed**, **Acceleration**, **Scroll speed**, **Natural scrolling**, **Tap to click**, **Vibrate on clicks**: how the touchpad feels. Speeds go from 0.3× to 3×.

**Receive files**: default on.

**Show in the share menu**: default on. Adds **Send to computer** to other apps' share sheets. The entry only exists once a computer is paired, so it never appears for someone who does not use the tool.

With more than one computer paired, **Send to computer** asks which one. Computers that are still connecting are listed greyed out and become selectable as they connect.

Only files another app shares from its own storage go through. A share that names a file inside WM Keyboard's own storage is ignored, so an app cannot use **Send to computer** to send out the keyboard's settings or clipboard history.

**Report battery level** and **Offer this phone's media**: both default on. Media needs the same notification access as the [media controls](/tools/media/) tool, and does nothing without it.

**Send one-time codes**: default off. A verification code that arrives in a notification goes to the paired computer, the code alone and never the message around it. WM Keyboard for the desktop offers it above the cursor; a KDE Connect desktop shows it as a notification. It needs **Codes from notifications** on under <SettingsPath path="Typing" />, which is what reads them.

Notifications for a pair request, a file or a ping that arrives while the keyboard is hidden, and the one shown while clipboard sharing is paused, have their own switch under <SettingsPath path="Notifications" />.

## Details and edge cases

- Everything stays on your local network. There is no account and no server. Both ends encrypt the connection with TLS, and each side pins the other's certificate at pairing.
- Passwords and one-time codes are never sent on their own, even with clipboard sending on. Nothing is sent from a password field, or while [incognito](/privacy/incognito/) pauses the clipboard. **Send › Clipboard** still sends whatever is there, because you asked.
- A password or code from the computer is marked sensitive on the phone, the way a password manager marks one, so Android 13 and later hide it in the copy preview and clipboard history treats it as a secret.
- Clipboard sync is separate from clipboard history. Turning history off does not stop the sync, and the other way round.
- Computers you have not paired with are only contacted while a device list is open, in the panel or in settings. The rest of the time the keyboard only talks to computers it already knows.
- The tool is hidden on the lock screen and before the first unlock after a restart. A computer cannot type into a locked phone.
- The phone's key and the paired computers' certificates are left out of backups on purpose. Restored onto another phone they would put two devices on your network under one identity. Pair again on a new phone.
- The scroll direction and speed can differ between desktops, because the protocol does not define a scale. Both are settings for that reason.
- Text, links and clipboard only. Images on the clipboard are not synced. Send them as files.
- Not supported: SMS and calls, notification mirroring, browsing the phone's files from the computer, and ringing the phone. Those need permissions a keyboard should not hold. The KDE Connect app does them well, and the two can run together.
